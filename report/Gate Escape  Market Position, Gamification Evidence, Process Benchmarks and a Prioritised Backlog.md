# Gate Escape: Market Position, Gamification Evidence, Process Benchmarks and a Prioritised Backlog

Gate Escape is a single-verb, blueprint-styled block-escape puzzle: 40 solver-verified levels in four sheets of ten, three stars only at solver-proven par, no clock anywhere, no randomness at runtime, no accounts, no server, no monetization, plus a daily identical "Daily Draft", a weekly pick-2-of-4 "Field Survey", cosmetic paper unlocks at 24 of 30 stars per sheet, and an order-constrained "approval chain" mechanic on Sheet 4. This report answers what that design is worth against the market, which proven mechanics can be imported without breaking its hard constraints, how its build/test/review factory compares with studio practice, what to instrument, and what to do next — in ranked order.

Every figure below carries the link it was read from. Where the evidence does not exist, that is stated rather than filled in. Recommendations that rest on judgement rather than evidence are labelled as judgement.

## 1. Executive summary — the ten findings that should change decisions

| # | Finding | The number behind it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The paid-acquisition path is arithmetically closed and should be abandoned as a plan, not deferred. | Casual-puzzle CPI is $2.10 US iOS / $0.85 US Android ([RocketShip HQ summary of Unity/Liftoff](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/unity-liftoff-mobile-gaming-report-2025-summary/)) against $0.03–$0.10 casual/puzzle ARPDAU ([Juego Studio](https://www.juegostudio.com/blog/arpdau-benchmarks-by-game-genre)) and 2% puzzle D30 ([Adjust](https://www.adjust.com/blog/puzzle-games-trends-strategies/)). |
| 2 | The daily puzzle is the product, not the meta — it is the only mechanic in the entire evidence base with 80%+ week-over-week retention. | LinkedIn reports "84% of people who play today will play again tomorrow" and "80% will still be playing a week from now" ([LinkedIn news](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/linkedin-announces-zip-)), versus a 3.42–3.94% median D7 across 11,600 games ([GameAnalytics 2025 Benchmarks](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2025-mobile-gaming-benchmarks)). |
| 3 | The deterministic reward economy costs nothing in engagement — the "variable rewards win" folklore only holds when the variable range never reaches zero. | With a binary 0/20 schedule at the same mean, fixed rewards were rated *more* enjoyable, *t*₁₆₀ = −3.40, *p* < 0.001, and with 0/50 fixed won on both choice and survey ([Kao et al.](https://people.csail.mit.edu/dkao/pdf/kao2012rewardpreference.pdf)). |
| 4 | The single highest-value retention change available is decoupling the daily habit unit from the difficulty goal — which Gate Escape has already done and must never undo. | Duolingo's decoupling test (streak extended by one lesson, not a hard XP goal) produced +3.3% D14, +1% DAU, +19% streak rate among new learners, and over +40% in learners on a 7+ day streak ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak/)). |
| 5 | Par-based difficulty measured by an optimal solver is not human difficulty; the asserted sawtooth may not be a sawtooth for players. | The strongest predictor of human completion rate is the move count of roughly the best 5% of agent runs, not the optimum ([arXiv:2306.14626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14626)), and difficulty is better modelled as the distribution of in-level actions ([CoG 2021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/CoG52621.2021.9619050)). |
| 6 | The rescue ad sits on the one moment that must stay comparable, and every published precedent either prices the assist in the shared currency or voids the record. | Apple's Emoji Game clue "will count toward the player's total number of moves" ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/07/apple-news-plus-introduces-emoji-game/)); Apple's Reveal means the puzzle "won't count toward your stats, and completing the puzzle won't start or continue a streak" ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/find-puzzles-in-apple-news-iph4883822da/ios)). |
| 7 | Nobody has ever played this game except its author and its bots, and that is now the largest single risk in the project. | Six players per user type is the standard for finding usability problems ([GamesUserResearch.com](https://gamesuserresearch.com/how-many-players-do-i-need-for-a-playtest/)), obtainable at about $69 per participant with a TestFlight link ([platform round-up](https://gamesuserresearch.com/top-remote-playtest-platforms-for-unmoderated-testing/), [PlaytestCloud](https://www.playtestcloud.com/single-session-playtest)), and expert heuristics are documented to find *different* issues than user testing rather than the same ones ([Desurvire et al.](https://www.valuesatplay.org/wp-content/uploads/2007/09/desurvireplayabilityheurist.pdf)). |
| 8 | 40 levels is a D3–D7 test, not a D14+ test, and the content ceiling caps what any soft launch can prove. | SayGames' operating rule is 100 levels for a first public version and 200 before looking at D14+ ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/how-saygames-uses-game-analytics-to-balance-difficulty-in-hybrid-puzzle-games/)); reading D1 near 30% to ±3 pp at 95% confidence needs 896 installs per cell ([Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2025-12-16-mobile-soft-launch-complete-guide/)). |
| 9 | The "no dark patterns" posture is a scarce, third-party-verifiable market claim rather than a slogan. | Only 161 of 1,496 rated mobile games — 10.76% — had zero reported dark patterns across 85,388 rated instances ([arXiv 2412.05039](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05039v1)), against a $245M FTC order over dark patterns ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/03/ftc-finalizes-order-requiring-fortnite-maker-epic-games-pay-245-million-tricking-users-making)) and EU CPC Key Principles adopted in March 2025 ([European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/european-commission-hosts-stakeholders-talks-application-cpc-networks-key-principles-games-virtual-2025-06-03_en)). |
| 10 | The commercially honest revenue frame for a deterministic logic puzzle is thousands of dollars per week, with Mekorama — not Patrick's Parabox — as the pricing model. | 2025's chart-visible deterministic logic puzzles cleared roughly $8K–$40K peak weekly US revenue ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q4-ios-top-5-puzzle-riddle-games-revenue-us-64bbe65de1714cfff1a57ed8)); Mekorama runs free, ad-free, pay-what-you-want $0.99–$31.99 on ~50 dioramas to 10M+ Play downloads and 4.7★ on 58.9K iOS ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mekorama/id1079464948), [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.martinmagni.mekorama&hl=en_US)); a $0.99 gate cut Unblock Me's rating base from 539K to 2.4K in this exact niche ([free](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/unblock-me/id315019111), [premium](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/unblock-me-premium/id315021242)). |

Three of these are decision reversals rather than confirmations. Finding 5 says the difficulty curve the bot asserts is unproven for humans and needs a stochastic estimator before the level order is treated as settled. Finding 6 says the rewarded rescue inside a recorded daily attempt is the one monetization surface in the design that is at odds with the design's own integrity claims. Finding 7 says another 109 regression checks would buy less risk reduction than six paid playtesters.

## 2. Where Gate Escape sits in the market

### 2.1 Three clusters, and which one is the peer set

### 2.4 The white space, stated precisely

Against the daily-puzzle incumbents — NYT with 11.1 billion puzzle plays in 2024 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Games)) and more than 11.2 billion in 2025 ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/new-york-times-wordle-games-app-4ab76097d6155a022f089d03e94807c3)), LinkedIn with 7 games and 3,500 puzzles published inside a 1.2B-member network ([LinkedIn News](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/two-years-seven-games-countless-stories-connections-linkedin-news-mwyoe), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/12/linkedin-mini-sudoku-games.html)), and Puzzmo at 600,000 active players across 150 publications ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/hearst-plan-take-on-new-york-times-in-puzzles-wordle-2024-5)) — two Gate Escape properties are genuinely unclaimed in the fetched sources:

1. **Solver-proven par as the star metric.** No comparable in the escape/sliding cluster advertises provable optimality; the monetized incumbents structurally cannot, because they sell hints. "Every star is a proof, not a purchase" is a line the whole cluster is unable to copy.
2. **Player-chosen weekly contracts plus a banked, free, auto-applied freeze economy.** NYT, LinkedIn, Puzzmo, Apple News+ and Netflix all publish a fixed daily set with no player-selected variety; LinkedIn is the only one with a documented freeze spec at all ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6296670)).

Puzzmo is the encouraging precedent for reachable scale: 600,000 active players, two-thirds of revenue from a $39/year subscription, one-third from advertising, and a launch that spent **zero dollars** on ads while drawing 932,000 access attempts and 104,000 players through an invite-key mechanic ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/hearst-plan-take-on-new-york-times-in-puzzles-wordle-2024-5), [INMA](https://www.inma.org/blogs/ideas/post.cfm/hearst-cracks-the-engagement-code-with-puzzmo)). That is the acquisition playbook available to a solo developer; paid UA is not.

## 3. Feature-by-feature: Gate Escape against the incumbents

Verdict key: **Ahead** = better than the fetched comparables on the design's own terms; **Parity** = matches established practice; **Behind** = a documented practice exists that Gate Escape lacks; **Deliberately different** = a considered divergence with a cost worth naming.

| Gate Escape system | What top puzzle and trending games do | Verdict | The consequence to act on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Par + 3 stars at solver-proven optimum** | Star systems are widespread; provable optimality is not advertised by any fetched escape/sliding comparable. NYT Letter Boxed is the closest published par mechanic: "The total stated at game completion is a par-score… the goal is to try to match or best that score" ([NYT Help](https://help.nytimes.com/360011158491-New-York-Times-Games/28527193547028-Letter-Boxed)). Waffle is the mechanical twin: solve in 15 swaps, "Every WAFFLE can be solved in a minimum of 10 swaps", a star per swap remaining ([Waffle](https://wafflegame.net/)) | **Ahead** | Lead marketing with it. Three stars roughly doubled level recompletion (15.57% → 35.35%, χ² = 20.95, *p* < .001) and cut median extra moves above optimal from 2.69 to 0.67 ([PMC5659622](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5659622/)). |
| **Free, unlimited undo that refunds the move** | Baba Is You makes undo/restart the core failure valve — levels are never explicitly failed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baba_Is_You)); LinkedIn Queens layers Hint, Auto-check, Auto-place X's, Undo, Clear ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6269510)) | **Parity** (ahead of the monetized cluster) | Keep. In Unblock Me the equivalent is a paid ladder to $18.99 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/unblock-me/id315019111)). |
| **Hint behind a simulated rewarded ad** | Unblock Me sells hints in nine tiers and doubles the daily grant for a rewarded ad ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kiragames.unblockmefree&hl=en_US)); Apple's Emoji Game instead charges its clue in moves ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/07/apple-news-plus-introduces-emoji-game/)); Apple's Reveal voids stats and streaks ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/find-puzzles-in-apple-news-iph4883822da/ios)); Strands tracks "Solved Without Hints" as a first-class stat ([NYT Help](https://help.nytimes.com/360011158491-New-York-Times-Games/28214352967700-Strands)) | **Behind on record integrity** | The ad tag is fine; the missing piece is pricing. Charge the hint in the shared currency (moves) or mark the record, so an assisted result never reads as a clean one. |
| **Rescue: +3 moves once per attempt via ad, board preserved** | Parking Jam sells extra moves, extra time and 15-second timer extensions for ads ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/parking-jam-3d/id1498229533?see-all=reviews)); "additional moves" is the #2 highest-engagement rewarded reward type at 30.5%, just behind gacha at 31.1% ([Mistplay](https://business.mistplay.com/resources/rewarded-ads-stats)); context-sensitive placements such as "player runs out of resources" reach 38.1% engagement vs 23.8% between-level ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/unity-global-rewarded-ad-engagement-rose-by-32-in-2023/)) | **Parity in campaign, Behind in the recorded daily** | The placement is the best-performing rewarded surface in the industry, and Apple explicitly permits it ("Apps may otherwise incentivize users to take specific actions within apps (e.g. completing a level, watching an ad)" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Keep it in the campaign; in the recorded draft it must be priced and printed, never invisible. |
| **Certification at 24/30 stars → deterministic cosmetic** | Royal Match's collection is 15 sets × 9 cards = 135 cards in a 27-day window with random card drops and social trading ([Mechanics Playbook](https://gamification.gamebizconsulting.com/case-studies/royal-match/)); collections are documented as a top event type ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/albums-battle-passes-and-milestones-mobiles-top-event-types-to-introduce-this-summer/)) | **Deliberately different, and correct** | Retiring the word "chest" was right. Fixed rewards carry no engagement penalty when the alternative includes zero outcomes ([Kao et al.](https://people.csail.mit.edu/dkao/pdf/kao2012rewardpreference.pdf)). The threshold, though, is unmeasured — see §8. |
| **Four papers/themes with machine-checked contrast per paper** | Apple's accessibility labels include "Sufficient Contrast", "Dark Interface" and "Differentiate Without Color Alone" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels/)) | **Ahead** | Contrast floors enforced in CI are rarer than the guidelines' Basic tier implies ([GAG Basic](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/basic/)). Convert it into store-page labels. |
| **Daily Draft: one identical board a day, own par, one recorded attempt, unlimited practice** | Wordle: same word worldwide, streak needs a solve before local midnight ([NYT Help](https://help.nytimes.com/360011158491-New-York-Times-Games/24611727334932-Wordle)); NYT Mini: "Solve time is calculated the **first** time that you solve a puzzle" and "The timer will restart if the entire puzzle is cleared" ([NYT Help](https://help.nytimes.com/360011158491-New-York-Times-Games/360025912452-The-Mini-Crossword)); Waffle's paid packs "do not count towards your regular statistics" ([Waffle](https://wafflegame.net/)); Connections blocks replay entirely ([NYT Help](https://help.nytimes.com/360011158491-New-York-Times-Games/28525912587924-Connections)) | **Parity with the best of the category** | The record-once-then-practice model is directly precedented on both halves. Ship the day-boundary rule in plain words in-product, because it is the category's most-documented support question. |

## 4. Gamification principles worth importing

Each principle below is stated as evidence, effect size where published, then a concrete implementation that respects the hard constraints: no timers, no randomness, colour never the only signal, no dark patterns.

### 4.1 Endowed progress — the strongest single field result available

Ten-stamp cards with two stamps pre-filled were redeemed by 34% of customers against 19% for eight-stamp cards with none pre-filled, χ²(1) = 8.1, *p* < .01, with endowed customers averaging 2.9 days less between visits, *F*(1,636) = 5.2, *p* < .05, and inter-visit time shrinking 0.5 days per additional purchase, *F*(1,636) = 18.3 ([Nunes & Drèze](https://msbfile03.usc.edu/digitalmeasures/jnunes/intellcont/Endowed%20Progress%20Effect-1.pdf)). The work required was identical in both conditions; only the framing changed, which is why it stays inside the no-dark-patterns line as long as the requirement is stated honestly.

**Implementation.** Express the survey as "7 stamps to complete — 2 already stamped for showing up" rather than "5 to go", and express certification as 30 stars with the sheet's already-earned stars banked rather than as "24 needed, N remaining". Also pre-select the easiest offered contract as a worked example — which the build already does, ranked by expected clears and never a contract that can score zero on a clear.

### 4.2 Goal gradient — make remaining distance small-numbered and always visible

Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng's resurrection of the goal-gradient hypothesis is the canonical demonstration that effort accelerates as a reward nears, in café-stamp-card and online-rating field settings, with illusory progress and retention following ([Journal of Marketing Research](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39)); exact effect sizes were not obtainable from the fetched abstract.

**Implementation.** The certification chip should always read a small remaining number ("6 stars to the Night vellum"), never a ratio. The survey marks at 3/7/12/20 are already a goal-gradient ladder; the ⌖ at 20 is correct as a single terminal target.

### 4.3 Autonomy and self-determination — choice is the cheapest need to satisfy

SDT is the dominant lens in games HCI, though a review of 110 CHI and CHI PLAY papers found the field applies it selectively rather than testing it ([Tyack & Mekler](https://pure.itu.dk/ws/files/95235949/Tyack_2020_.pdf)); the foundational games application is Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski ([PDF](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf)). Competence is served by skill–challenge balance, which predicts flow in a real match-3 game ([PMC8943660](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8943660/)). Relatedness is structurally absent with no accounts.

**Implementation.** Pick-2-of-4 already serves autonomy; add a free swap until first progress plus one paid-in-effort swap afterwards, following Candy Crush's up-to-three-swaps-with-visible-rewards precedent ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2024/9/23/daily-missions-in-puzzles-why-should-we-see-them-more-often)). For competence, keep displaying the par delta on every result. For relatedness, the deterministic daily is the only serverless substitute available and it already exists.

### 4.4 Flow and difficulty — too easy is the measured risk, not too hard

Manipulating difficulty inside Candy Crush Saga by level complexity rather than speed — the no-timer case exactly — produced boredom means of 0.99 (easy) / 0.64 (regular) / 0.58 (hard), frustration 1.73 / 3.65 / 4.27, flow 2.62 / 2.83 / 2.83 and urge-to-play 6.84 / 7.58 / 7.70. Boredom *F*(1.44, 85.02) = 12.63, *p* < .001, η²p = 0.176; frustration *F*(2,118) = 134.57, *p* < .001, η²p = 0.695; flow *F*(1.65, 97.37) = 5.82, *p* = .007, η²p = 0.09; easy-vs-regular flow differed at *p* = .006 but regular-vs-hard did not, *p* = .922 ([PMC8943660](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8943660/)). A Halo 3 re-analysis of 42 players over 168 round-level observations found enjoyment maps onto *enjoyed* difficulty and *enjoyed* concentration rather than raw difficulty ([PMC5954478](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5954478/)).

**Implementation.** Flow plateaus between regular and hard while frustration keeps climbing with a very large effect, so the exam at L20 is defensible but the *frustration* cost is real and must be measured as satisfaction after a hard clear, not just clear rate. Because par is provable, the challenge is legible — "your best 9, par 7" converts a fail into a graded competence signal, which is the load-bearing element in this literature.

### 4.5 Fixed versus variable rewards — the finding that protects the whole design

Kao et al. compared fixed 10-point rewards against variable schedules with identical means, N per condition 173–707: uniform 5–15 was preferred over fixed (2.87 vs 2.13 world-choices, *t*₁₅₈ = 3.46, *p* < 0.001), binary 5/15 was preferred (*t*₄₅₉ = 2.01, *p* < 0.05), uniform 0–20 showed no preference (*t*₁₈₄ = 0.09, *p* = 0.93), binary 0/20 flipped to fixed being rated more enjoyable (*t*₁₆₀ = −3.40, *p* < 0.001), and binary 0/50 preferred fixed on both choice and survey (*t*₁₇₃ = −2.72, *p* < 0.01; *t*₁₆₂ = −3.61, *p* < 0.001) ([Kao et al.](https://people.csail.mit.edu/dkao/pdf/kao2012rewardpreference.pdf)). Individual differences were large: 119 strongly preferred variable and 84 strongly preferred fixed in the 5/15 condition.

**Implementation.** Vary reward *magnitude*, never *presence*. Every clear pays at least one star's worth of progress and every par clear pays more, which is already the design. This is the strongest evidence that the no-loot-box economy pays no engagement penalty, and it should be stated as such rather than defended apologetically.

### 4.6 Streaks and streak insurance — keep the unit trivial and the insurance free

| Mechanism | Published effect | Source |
|---|---|---|

| Decoupling the streak from the daily goal | +3.3% D14, +1% DAU, +10.5% streak population in 20 days, +19% among new learners, 7+ day streaks up over 40% | [Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak/) |

| Streak Wager (spend currency to bet) | Significant D1/D7/D14 gains, D7 largest at +14% | [Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals/) |
| Outage protection at operator scale | "BRB has protected over 2 million streaks", reducing support load | [Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/protecting-streaks-from-site-issues/) |

**Implementation.** The day spine already stamps on any clear, which is the correct side of the decoupling experiment; the failure mode to legislate against permanently is ever requiring a par clear for a stamp. Keep freezes earned, capped, non-expiring and auto-applied — the LinkedIn spec Gate Escape already matches ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6296670)) — and add a locally auditable "day was broken" flag so a bad build can retroactively excuse a miss, following Duolingo's outage practice. Do **not** copy the Streak Wager despite its +14% D7: it is a wager. The widget is the highest-value zero-server retention surface available and is fully deterministic; note the retention figure itself is unpublished, so treat the size of the win as unknown.

### 4.7 A purity token instead of assist counters

LinkedIn prints one word under stated conditions — "'Flawless' is displayed if no hints or reveals are used, and auto-place-Xs are turned off" for Queens, and "if you didn't use any backtracks or hints" for Zip — and voids it when tutorial scaffolding pre-fills the board ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6889085)). Strands elevates "Solved Without Hints" to a first-class stat alongside the streak ([NYT Help](https://help.nytimes.com/360011158491-New-York-Times-Games/28214352967700-Strands)).

**Implementation.** Replace "undo 0 · hint 0" in the FIELD REPORT with a single token — CLEAN — printed only when undo = 0, hint = 0 and rescue = false, and omitted otherwise. This is more shareable, more legible to a non-player, and strictly more honest than two counters that most readers cannot rank.

### 4.8 Collections without drops

Royal Match's collection is 15 sets × 9 cards, unlocking at level 41, with one teammate card request per 24 hours ([Mechanics Playbook](https://gamification.gamebizconsulting.com/case-studies/royal-match/)) — random acquisition plus social trading, both blocked here. Collections are nonetheless documented as a top event type ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/albums-battle-passes-and-milestones-mobiles-top-event-types-to-introduce-this-summer/)) and a retention device ([GameRefinery](https://www.gamerefinery.com/attracting-and-retaining-players-with-collection-systems/)).

**Implementation.** A "case file" album where slot *n* fills when level *n* is cleared at par, plus a small block of slots for daily par clears, and the tutorial slots pre-filled to stack with endowed progress. Zero randomness, zero purchase, same completion drive. This is judgement rather than evidence at the effect-size level — no fetched source quantifies deterministic-collection lift.

### 4.9 Social proof without leaderboards

Small pools motivate more than large ones: "Ranking 8th out of 20 players is more motivating than 8,000th out of 10,000" ([GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/crack-the-match-3-code-part-2)). A lab study (N = 111 after exclusions, 5 rounds, 10-player boards, score gap held at 8–12 points) manipulated rank at 1st/2nd versus 9th/10th, but the directional outcome was not extractable from the fetched page, so "low rank demotivates" remains plausible and unconfirmed ([Internet Research 33(7)](https://www.emerald.com/intr/article/33/7/1/178330/How-leaderboard-positions-shape-our-motivation-the)). What exists in the wild is the average-player comparator: CineNerdle prints "You" against "Avg" per daily puzzle — average swaps 12.0 and 11.1, average checks 4.2, average attempts 3.8 ([CineNerdle](https://www.cinenerdle.app/)) — and Connections Bot rates performance against "data from millions of players" ([NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/08/27/upshot/connections-bot-faq.html)).

**Implementation.** Ship a personal board first — your par-versus-best history per level — because it needs no server at all. If the beacon is ever enabled, publish a single anonymous aggregate per daily board and print "You 9 · Avg 11" on the result card. That delivers Puzzmo's own stated goal, "we want to give Puzzmo a feel that people are playing around you", without the leaderboards they now regret defaulting to ([Puzzmo](https://blog.puzzmo.com/posts/2026/02/06/follows-not-friends/)).

### 4.10 Two ideas to stop citing

**Zeigarnik.** The 2025 meta-analysis is decisive against the strong form: pooled ratio of recalled interrupted-to-completed tasks 0.99 across 38 publications, interrupted share of total recalled 49.43%, Cohen's *d_z* = 0.15, and the effect appearing only in the relaxed condition (0.96 neutral / 0.88 achievement / 1.07 relaxed) ([Nature HSSC](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05000-w)). What survives is Ovsiankina: about 67% of interrupted tasks get resumed ([same](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05000-w)) — which justifies the existing "Resume level N" affordance rather than any deliberate teasing of incompletion.

**Near miss.** Repeated replication failure across half a century: neither of Reid's 1986 replications produced significant effects, only 1 of Ghezzi et al.'s 3 replications showed a main effect, Kassinove & Schare found persistence peaking non-monotonically at 30% near-miss density, and pigeons consistently *preferred* alternatives with no near misses ([Springer review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10899-019-09891-8)). The fail sheet's honest reading of the true position — "the last block is one drag from its gate", computed by the engine — is feedback, not a manufactured near miss, and is the right design.

### 4.11 What the meta-analytic base does and does not license

Two independent meta-analyses agree on direction and rough magnitude: Hedges' *g* = 0.654, 95% CI [0.442, 0.866], *I²* = 88.92% across 41 K–12 studies ([Wiley 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/pits.70056)), and *g* = 0.822, 95% CI [0.567, 1.078], *I²* = 94.09% across 41 studies and >5,071 participants ([Frontiers 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10591086/)). Richer designs beat thin ones: mechanics-only *g* = 0.533, mechanics + dynamics *g* = 0.997, mechanics + dynamics + aesthetics *g* = 1.285, *Q*between = 19.05, *p* = 0.004 ([Frontiers](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10591086/)). Both are educational studies of gamified non-games; no fetched source gives a meta-analytic effect size for gamification on mobile-game retention. The honest read: the stars-plus-paper-unlock-plus-narrative-framing stack ("papers", "approval chain", "weather delays") is the design *shape* with the largest measured effect, not an over-engineering risk — but the transfer to a retention curve is an inference. Octalysis, by contrast, has no controlled validation in the fetched corpus ([CEUR](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3147/paper8.pdf), [Nature HSSC](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-023-02243-3)) and should be used only as a coverage checklist.

### 5.2 The share-string redesign, concretely

Current format prints five information rows. The evidence says a share string must be rankable at a glance on **one** axis — Wordle by fewer rows, Waffle by more stars, LinkedIn by lower time plus "Flawless" ([Slate](https://slate.com/human-interest/2023/07/nyt-new-york-times-connections-game-worldle.html), [Waffle](https://wafflegame.net/), [LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6889085)). Proposed:

```
GATE ESCAPE · DRAFT 3 SEP · #003
★★☆  8/7
■■■■■■■□
CLEAN
```

The primary axis is `moves/par`; the bar is the same encoding as today; `CLEAN` replaces the undo and hint counters and appears only when nothing assisted the run; a rescued run prints `RESCUED` in its place and adds the rescue moves into the total. Route % moves to the in-app result card only — it is a second axis, and a second axis destroys glanceable comparability. The day number is added because it is the anti-desync device recommended below. The codepoint allowlist stays as pinned.

### 5.3 Content-integrity risks specific to 365 precomputed boards

Two documented Wordle incidents were both silent divergence between cached clients and canon. When NYT replaced an answer, "only those who had refreshed their browser window would have seen the new, less controversial answer" ([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-us-canada-61339177)); after the migration, players who had not refreshed got different words entirely, and "The loss of this shared experience and the fact that there are now competing versions has angered fans" ([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-60416057)). A single broken puzzle day produced reactions like "spoiled the best hour of my week" from solvers "on the verge of tears" while the online grid was correct — a pure version-mismatch failure ([Vulture](https://www.vulture.com/article/new-york-times-crossword-error-reactions.html)).

Gate Escape's append-only lock file is already the right primitive. Three additions follow from the evidence: ship a day→board-hash manifest and fail loudly on mismatch rather than silently mapping; print the day number in the FIELD REPORT so a divergent client is diagnosable from a screenshot; and build the correction mechanism *before* it is needed, so a bad day can be fixed without desyncing installed clients. Also freeze the scoring contract before day 1 — NYT changed Wordle's statistics formula mid-flight ("As of 2025, we updated the statistics calculation to include archive play"), which is exactly the retroactive change that generates support load ([NYT Help](https://help.nytimes.com/360011158491-New-York-Times-Games/24611727334932-Wordle)).

On leaks: an entire SEO industry publishes daily hints and answers, including major outlets ([CNET example](https://www.cnet.com/tech/gaming/todays-nyt-connections-hints-answers-and-help-for-dec-13-916/)), and the temptation is documented as arriving after three failed guesses ([Machined](https://machined.substack.com/p/todays-wordle-answer-is-everything)). NYT's countermeasure is to own the surface itself, publishing Companion columns dated after the puzzle and holding Spelling Bee answers until "the following day once it becomes a past puzzle" ([NYT Help](https://help.nytimes.com/360011158491-New-York-Times-Games/23675493312404-Spelling-Bee), [Connections Companion](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/17/crosswords/connections-companion-1133.html)). For a par-based board with many valid routes to par, a leak is structurally less damaging than a one-word answer — but a route video is still a leak, and the existing no-coordinates rule is the correct and sufficient countermeasure inside the product.

Finally, human review of the 365 boards is not optional. NYT pays testers on all games to catch "potentially incorrect or offensive puzzles" and grids where "there could be multiple correct solves" ([Vox](https://www.vox.com/culture/2024/3/26/24111847/connections-nyt-game-hint-word-puzzles)). The par-game analogue is: verify par is genuinely optimal (already asserted) **and** that par+3 is achievable by a plausible non-optimal human route — which the current pipeline does not test, because it only replays the optimal line.

The evidence base here is thinner than for streaks, and the honest summary is that the design is already ahead of its daily-puzzle competitors and roughly at parity with match-3 practice.

| Question | What the evidence says | Change to make |

| Do player-chosen weekly goals beat fixed daily quests? | No head-to-head experiment exists in the fetched corpus. ## 7. The approval chain and late rule changes

### 7.1 Precedents

| Game | Order constraint | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Railbound** (2022) | "requires numbered cars to couple in numerical order, and introduces junctions, crossing barriers, and tunnels progressively"; 240+ levels; **2023 Apple Design Award for interaction** | The single best precedent: the same rule shape, mass-market casual audience, and an Apple award for the interaction ([Puzzlebyrinth](https://puzzlebyrinth.com/fr/articles/track-laying-puzzle-design)) |
| **Cosmic Express** | "encodes pickup and drop-off order into the shape of a single track because seats are limited and the track cannot cross itself"; difficulty from "the combinatorial explosion of possibilities in a single line, rather than from board size" | Order constraints multiply difficulty without enlarging the board — the right tool for a small grid ([same](https://puzzlebyrinth.com/fr/articles/track-laying-puzzle-design)) |
| **Trainyard** (2010) / **Freeways** (2017) | Binary true/false judgement versus graded evaluation "by degree" of traffic throughput | Gate Escape's par/star system is the graded form layered on a binary solve — the stronger combination ([same](https://puzzlebyrinth.com/fr/articles/track-laying-puzzle-design)) |
| **Sort-puzzle family** (Block Jam 3D, Hexa Sort, Magic Sort) | Dock-slot scarcity forces sequencing: "With only a few slots available, players are forced into real decisions"; "You can't keep everything 'in progress' forever" | Confirms sequencing tension is commercially proven at scale ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/6/sort-puzzles-how-a-new-subgenre-is-born)) |
| **Parking Jam 3D** | Ordered-exit car unblocking at 100M+ downloads ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=parking.jam3d.traffic.jam.games.car.puzzle&hl=en_US)) | Mass-market validation of the shape; no published order-rule design documentation exists |

Three transferable rules follow. First, introduce the constraint pieces one per level, because Railbound's "gradual introduction of pieces makes the reading learned in one level the premise of the next" ([Puzzlebyrinth](https://puzzlebyrinth.com/fr/articles/track-laying-puzzle-design)) — which Gate Escape does, chain length 2 → 3 → 4 with chain cost rising from 0 to +3 by L40. Second, scarcity is a built-in hint: "a limited number of track pieces functions as a de facto hint: fewer available pieces narrow the possible solution shapes while illuminating the search space" ([same](https://puzzlebyrinth.com/fr/articles/track-laying-puzzle-design)) — which L31's 4 blocks / 2 colours / chain 2 already exploits. Third, the graded-on-binary structure is already the stronger of the two published options.

### 7.2 How far a late rule change can go

This is the weakest-evidenced question in the entire brief, and it should be treated as such.

- **A quantified tolerance threshold does not exist.** No fetched source provides a figure for how many casual players churn when a new rule appears after level N.
- **The most citable constraint is the new-mechanic rule:** "If you introduce new mechanics at a certain level, they need to be easy to explain – and you need to give the player unchallenging opportunities to use them when they first appear" ([GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/match-3-games-metrics-guide)). L31 at par 4 / limit 8 with the chain matching the natural order satisfies this exactly, and the temporary limit relaxation (par+4 on L31–32, par+3 on L33–34) is the correct instrument.
- **A late rule change is effectively a third disclosure layer** if the player must also relearn UI, and beyond two layers usability drops because "users often get lost" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)). The mitigation Gate Escape uses — the approval-chain legend row appearing only once Sheet 4 is reachable, plus the one-shot toast, plus the dashed 1→2→3 polyline drawn once — is a good implementation of the two-layer rule. The solid-stamp-plus-chevron for next-up and outlined-at-reduced-alpha for waiting is also a correct non-colour channel.
- **Mass-casual precedent supports adding systems late, staggered and signposted:** Royal Match added five systems between levels 22 and 41 and labelled its first "hard level" only at 39 ([Mechanics Playbook](https://gamification.gamebizconsulting.com/case-studies/royal-match/)). Honest labelling of hard levels is both anti-dark-pattern and precedented.
- **The diagnostic question on every failed playtest** is Hazelden's: "Are they just not getting the puzzle even though they have all the information or is there something the game is doing a poor job of teaching? In [the latter] case it's my fault and I need to fix it" ([Rock Paper Shotgun](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/cosmic-express-alan-hazelden)).
- **Academic evidence on mechanic-introduction order is not usable:** a bachelor thesis tested the 24 orderings of four mechanics with a planned 25 testers ([DiVA](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1769453/FULLTEXT01.pdf)) — exploratory only.

**Recommendation (part evidence, part judgement).** Keep the current teaching ramp. Add branching availability inside Sheet 4 — advance on any 8 of 10 — which is Hazelden's published mitigation for mis-tuned difficulty a solo dev cannot A/B away, alongside grouping levels into mechanic "constellations" ([Rock Paper Shotgun](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/cosmic-express-alan-hazelden)). Then instrument the introduction level and treat a spike in *quit* rate, not fail rate, as the kill signal, per the pass-rate-versus-quit-rate heuristic below. The judgement component is the 8-of-10 threshold; nothing in the evidence sets that number.

### 8.2 Optimal par versus human difficulty — the central modelling gap

The bot asserts the sawtooth profile so a future retune cannot flatten it. That is excellent engineering guarding a possibly wrong metric.

- The strongest published predictor of human completion rate in a mobile puzzle game is **the move count of roughly the best 5% of agent runs**, not the optimum, and agent-to-human differences across levels are highly correlated even though the agent does not reach human absolute performance ([arXiv:2306.14626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14626)).
- Probability-of-completion is itself too thin a definition; a parametric model of the **distribution of actions within a level** describes difficulty in the vast majority of *Lily's Garden* levels ([CoG 2021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/CoG52621.2021.9619050)).
- King's production bot deliberately picks the action a *human* would most likely take rather than the optimal one, trained on "millions and millions of states", validated by checking that bot difficulty estimates track real difficulty linearly — with 95% fewer manual tweaks and level tweaks 50% faster ([GamesIndustry.biz](https://www.gamesindustry.biz/how-king-is-using-ai-to-speed-up-development-of-new-candy-crush-levels)).
- Socialpoint models match-3 difficulty as win rate via a shifted negative binomial where `r` is the minimum moves needed, reporting 1.5 percentage points of average error per move increment ([Socialpoint](https://socialpoint-analytics.medium.com/tuning-level-difficulty-in-match-3-games-a-data-driven-framework-7b3cc07b2116)). This transfers unusually well here because `r` **is** solver-verified par, and the assumption Socialpoint flags as broken — that the distribution does not change when move count changes, because players spend boosters when close — holds better in Gate Escape, which has no boosters.
- The best available pre-release estimator is not simulation alone: models trained on **cohort statistics combined with simulated data** are most accurate in all scenarios, with artificial neural networks most consistent ([arXiv:2401.17436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17436)).

**The concrete change.** Add a stochastic/noisy-greedy player to the tools side and report, per level, the distribution of moves over many runs — specifically the best-5% move count and the 90th-percentile move count — then re-derive the difficulty profile from that rather than from par-minus-blocks. This needs no reinforcement learning and no ML; it needs a distribution instead of an optimum. If the human-estimated profile disagrees with the asserted sawtooth, the sawtooth assertion is the thing that is wrong.

### 8.3 Where the walls should sit, and quit rate versus pass rate

The single most useful frame in the difficulty literature is a heuristic, and it says to optimise the wrong metric from the one most designers watch: "A level with a **40% pass rate but a 1% quit rate** is well-designed", whereas "a level with a **60% pass rate but a 10% quit rate** is actually harder in the ways that matter", with a redesign floor at levels a simulated average player cannot win at least 3–5% of the time, and tuning aimed at the intermediate band that is "60-70% of active base" ([Ludaxis](https://www.ludaxis.io/blog/level-design-puzzle-games-guide)). All of that is studio-blog grade and should be labelled as such; no fetched primary source publishes a canonical target win rate for casual puzzle.

Measured attempt distributions are better evidenced. In a commercial mobile puzzle game across the first 500 of 6,000+ levels, steady-state difficulty runs about **3.2 attempts per completion** after roughly the first 100 levels, with a constant baseline of 3.23 for levels 100–400, easy levels typically completed in **1 attempt**, and hard levels averaging upwards of **7 attempts** with individual players sometimes exceeding 30 ([arXiv 2401.17436](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17436v1)). An operating studio maps this to fail rate directly: **50% fail rate is roughly 2 attempts per level and 80% fail rate roughly 5**, with the warning that an average of 2 starts can hide players needing 10–20 attempts and an average of 5 can hide players trying 50 times and still failing ([SayGames via PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/how-saygames-uses-game-analytics-to-balance-difficulty-in-hybrid-puzzle-games/)).

Two consequences are specific to Gate Escape. First, because nothing is random, the tail risk SayGames describes — unlucky players facing a nearly unbeatable board — is structurally absent, so attempts-per-level distributions should be tighter and a spike in the 90th percentile is a much cleaner difficulty signal than it is in a randomised match-3. That is a genuine analytical advantage of determinism and should be exploited. Second, the same source's churn warning applies with full force: "If 100 players entered a level and 90 moved on, it would seem that 10 players had dropped off" — but in harder puzzle games players stay on a level across sessions or days, so a point-in-time progression read misstates loss and churn must be attributed over time ([same](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/how-saygames-uses-game-analytics-to-balance-difficulty-in-hybrid-puzzle-games/)).

### 8.4 The certification threshold

24 of 30 stars is unmeasured, and a proposal to move it to 21 waits on a real star distribution. The evidence does not settle it, but two facts frame the decision. Stars strongly drive replay and solution quality — recompletion 15.57% → 35.35% and median extra moves above optimal 2.69 → 0.67 — but showed **no significant effect on returning** (33.96% vs 27.44%, n.s.) and did not increase levels completed ([PMC5659622](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5659622/)). And pressuring replay was counterproductive: the 3-STAR-R variant produced the best solution quality (0.67 extra moves) but *lower* recompletion (28.14%) than plain 3-STAR (35.35%) ([same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5659622/)) — letting the star gap speak for itself beat pushing, which is also the anti-dark-pattern choice and vindicates the current "Replay for ★★★" being an offer rather than a nag.

Ruling: hold 24/30 until the star distribution exists, then set the threshold so that the median player who plays every level in a sheet lands within one or two stars of it — because the goal-gradient effect depends on *perceived* proximity ([Kivetz et al.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39)). That is judgement; no source gives a threshold rule.

### 9.2 What the factory does that genuinely exceeds indie norms

Nine practices are verifiably above the norm: a completeness oracle proving all 40 levels solvable with par; byte-identical deterministic regeneration plus optimal-solution replay through the real engine; a differential rule-parity oracle against an independent implementation; a ~2-minute commit gate with 109 named checks; two-platform bot coverage; accessibility and motion checks inside CI; a mandated regression check per fix; zero-network and bundle-freshness assertions; and an adversarial AI persona filing structured defect reports. The comparison that makes this concrete: Riot needed roughly a year of multi-engineer work to reach gameplay determinism and the record/playback tooling it enables ([Riot](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/determinism-league-legends-implementation)), and modl.ai's marquee indie case study exists because a 20-person studio's single QA person could not cover branching content — where bots found states the player could not proceed from, complete freezes, and invisible buttons that retained interactive functionality, testing 60+ levels overnight from an end-of-day build ([modl.ai](https://modl.ai/testing-content-heavy-games-with-ai-bots)).

### 9.3 Ranked missing practices

1. **Any human player, ever** — six unmoderated sessions, about $414 at $69 each, or one free at Antidote ([round-up](https://gamesuserresearch.com/top-remote-playtest-platforms-for-unmoderated-testing/), [Antidote](https://antidote.gg/platform-features/)). Expert heuristics find *different* issues than user testing, not fewer ([Desurvire et al.](https://www.valuesatplay.org/wp-content/uploads/2007/09/desurvireplayabilityheurist.pdf)).
2. **Human-like/stochastic difficulty estimation** — days of work, no ML ([arXiv:2306.14626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14626), [CoG 2021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/CoG52621.2021.9619050)).
3. **Crash/ANR instrumentation with the vitals budget as an explicit gate**, and Crashlytics velocity alerts turned on, since they are off by default ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2022/10/raising-bar-on-technical-quality-on-google-play.html), [Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/docs/crashlytics/velocity-alerts)).
4. **Real-device matrix plus Game Loop wiring** — free on Play alpha/beta, and the existing autoplay flag already *is* a game loop, which also unlocks per-device average FPS ([Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/docs/test-lab/android/get-started), [Play Console](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844487?hl=en)).
5. **Play closed-testing compliance: 12 testers × 14 continuous days** ([Play Console](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en)).
6. **Phased release plan with pre-declared halt criteria** ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/update-your-app/release-a-version-update-in-phases/)).
7. **Multi-rater critic with decomposed severity and inter-rater agreement** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-rate-the-severity-of-usability-problems/)).
8. **Richer issue schema** — evidence artefact, build/device/OS/locale, causes ordered by impact, player-impact statement, heuristic reference, positives, reproducibility rate as hits over attempts plus seed, and a theme key ([Steve Bromley](https://www.stevebromley.com/blog/2017/03/08/whats-in-a-games-user-research-report/), [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-conduct-a-heuristic-evaluation/), [Play Console](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844487?hl=en)).
9. **Remote-config plumbing for level order, par, star bands and hint economics** ([Unity](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/remote-config)).
10. **Seeded fuzz/monkey soak testing** ([Android Monkey](https://www.dre.vanderbilt.edu/~schmidt/android/android-4.0/out/target/common/docs/doc-comment-check/guide/developing/tools/monkey.html)).
11. **Formal accessibility audit pass** with Scanner and Inspector beyond contrast and motion ([Android](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/testing), [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/accessibility-inspector)).
12. **Explicit heuristic-set mapping** (Nielsen 10, PLAY, GAG) driving the critic's rubric ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/), [Springer PLAY](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-02774-1_60)).
13. **Apple 2.1 pre-submission checklist as a CI artefact** ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/rejections/)).
14. **Effort estimates plus RICE/MoSCoW labels on triage, with SKIP items logged** ([Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/blog/rice-simple-prioritization-for-product-managers/), [Agile Business Consortium](https://www.agilebusiness.org/resource/what-is-moscow-prioritization/)).
15. **Soft-launch plan with pre-declared kill and re-tune criteria** — alpha tests the core loop and technology, beta tests the meta loop, UA, scalability and monetization; churned players cost 5–10× more to re-acquire; long-tail Android devices only appear at scale ([a16z](https://a16z.com/mobile-game-soft-launch/), [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/4-soft-launch-metrics-that-matter-to-the-success-of-your-mobile-game)).
16. **Post-launch fusion of cohort statistics with simulated data for difficulty modelling** — the natural end-state for the solver already built ([arXiv:2401.17436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17436)).

The one-line summary: the factory is stronger than typical indie practice on **correctness and determinism** and weaker than typical indie practice on **everything requiring a human or a device**. Six paid playtesters and a Test Lab run would close more risk than another 109 assertions.

## 10. Telemetry, KPIs and the soft-launch plan

### 10.1 The event catalogue is complete; the property layer is not

The shipped catalogue is already richer than most soft-launch minimum viable telemetry. What is missing are properties and conventions. Two rules govern the additions. Numeric payloads belong in a value field, because "It is important to use the value field for any dynamic data - e.g. time, damage, scores - in order to avoid cardinality issues" ([GameAnalytics](https://docs.gameanalytics.com/events-metrics-and-filtering/event-types/progression-events/)), and levels should use the progression construct rather than free-form design events ([GameAnalytics](https://docs.gameanalytics.com/events-metrics-and-filtering/event-types/design-events/)). And funnel-critical properties must appear on **every** step, because "Every event in the funnel must have that property for the holding constant feature to work" ([Amplitude](https://amplitude.com/docs/data/data-planning-playbook)).

| Layer | Additions |
|---|---|
| **Global (every event)** | `schema_v`, in-memory-only `session_id`, `session_num`, `event_seq`, `client_ts`, `build`, `platform`, `os_version`, coarse `device_class`, `locale`, store-level `country`, `is_supporter`, and `consent_analytics` as granted/denied/not_required |
| **Level funnel** (on `level_start`, `win`, `fail`, `restart`, `retry`, `undo`, `hint*`, `block_exit`, `rescue_*`, `replay`) | `level_id`, `level_index`, **`attempt_no`** — the single most important addition, because difficulty is operationalised as attempts to complete ([arXiv 2401.17436](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17436v1)) — plus `lifetime_attempts_on_level`, a single `result` enum, `moves_used`/`moves_par`/`moves_delta`, `duration_ms`, `ttfi_ms`, `undo_count`/`hint_count`/`restart_count`, `entry_source`, `first_clear`, `session_level_ordinal` |
| **Assistance funnel** | New `rescue_offer_shown` — today `rescue_offer_tap` has no denominator, which makes the highest-engagement rewarded surface unmeasurable ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/unity-global-rewarded-ad-engagement-rose-by-32-in-2023/)); plus `offer_reason`, `offer_shown_count`, `accepted`, and on the ad side `ad_request`, `ad_no_fill`, `ad_error`, `ad_reward_granted`, `fill_latency_ms`, `abandon_point_ms` |
| **Lifecycle** | `app_foreground`/`app_background`, because session start alone cannot compute session length reliably and GameAnalytics ships both automatically ([GameAnalytics](https://docs.gameanalytics.com/events-metrics-and-filtering/event-types/event-types-introduction/)); heartbeat gains `interval_s` and `state` and is suppressed when other events prove liveness, as Unity's `gameRunning` does at 60 seconds ([Unity](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/analytics/events/standard-events)); `notification_permission`/`notification_open`; `error_event`; **the missing `streak_lapse` event**; and `cohort_day` as a property on session start |
| **Daily and weekly** | `daily_*` gains `streak_len`, `puzzle_date`, `solved_in_moves`, `outcome`, `used_hint`; new `weekly_goal_*` with `goal_id`, `progress`, `target`, `completed` — weekly goals currently emit nothing usable for a funnel |
| **Monetization (before it is built)** | `store_open`, `offer_view`, `purchase_start`/`complete`/`fail`/`restore` mirroring GA4's required set of `transaction_id`, `items`, `currency`, `value` ([GA4 reference](https://developers.google.com/analytics/devguides/collection/ga4/reference/events)), `entitlement_changed`, `paywall_dismissed`, `cosmetic_unlock` |

`cohort_day` plus `session_num` is the workaround for having no persistent user id: a day-N *active-cohort* curve can be computed server-side without any per-user identifier. Transport should copy the documented GameAnalytics collector contract — gzipped JSON array flushed roughly every 20 seconds or on background, body under the 1 MB limit, retry only on offline or HTTP 413, wipe on 200/400/401 ([setup](https://docs.gameanalytics.com/event-tracking-and-integrations/sdks-and-collection-api/api/setup/), [limitations](https://docs.gameanalytics.com/event-tracking-and-integrations/sdks-and-collection-api/api/limitations/)) — and stay inside GA4's envelope of 500 distinct event names, 25 parameters per event, 40-character names and 100-character values so the same schema can be mirrored later without renaming ([GA4 limits](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9267744?hl=en)).

### 10.2 Privacy: what the beacon can and cannot do

| Question | Answer from the sources |
|---|---|
| ATT prompt needed? | **No**, provided no data is linked with other companies' data, nothing goes to data brokers and the IDFA is not accessed — Apple defines tracking as exactly those things and calls out third-party SDKs that combine data across developers' apps ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/)). This changes the moment a rewarded-ad SDK is added. |
| Privacy nutrition label? | **Yes.** "Collect" means transmitting data off device and retaining it beyond servicing the request, and on-device-only processing is out of scope ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)). The honest label is Usage Data / Product Interaction — Analytics — **Not Linked to You**, plus Diagnostics if crash data is sent. Declaring "Data Not Collected" while the beacon runs would be false. |
| Play Data Safety form? | **Yes**, and it is required on closed, open and production tracks; only internal-testing-only apps are exempt, and discrepancies may trigger enforcement action ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469?hl=en)). |
| Privacy manifest? | In practice yes — `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` is required if any required-reason API is touched, and a local save or install-id implementation almost certainly touches `UserDefaults`-style storage; declarations have been required for uploads since **1 May 2024**, and fingerprinting is prohibited regardless of ATT permission ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/privacy-manifest-files), [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/describing-use-of-required-reason-api), [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=3d8a9yyh)). |
| EU/UK consent prompt avoidable? | **Not established.** PECR regulation 6 "applies to anyone who stores information on a user's device or gains access to information on a user's device, in either case by any method", explicitly including apps, with only a strict-necessity exception that analytics does not obviously meet ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/cookies-and-similar-technologies/)). The audience-measurement exemption route via EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 and CNIL could not be verified. |
| Safest posture | Store no persistent analytics id at all: per-session UUID in memory only, no device identifiers, all cross-session aggregation server-side and cohort-level, and gate the whole beacon behind `consent_analytics` in EU/UK builds so denial suppresses transmission entirely rather than anonymising it. |
| Kids category? | **Avoid entirely.** Apple: "Apps in the Kids Category should not include third-party analytics or third-party advertising" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)); Play Families forbids transmitting AAID and similar identifiers from children or users of unknown age ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en)); COPPA treats persistent identifiers as personal information and covers passive tracking, not just active collection ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)). |

## 11. Monetization and launch

### 11.1 The model

The recommendation is the one the design already has pencilled in, and the evidence supports it: **free download, rewarded rescue and rewarded hint, one-time "Supporter Edition" that removes forced ads, fixed cosmetic paper folios, and paid expansion sheets only after validation.** Four evidence lines converge on it.

First, rewarded video is the only format whose measured properties fit a premium-feeling product: completion rates above 95% against 60–70% for forced formats ([Playio](https://blog.playio.co/rewarded-ad-benchmarks-2026)), the highest eCPM of any format on both platforms across a dataset of 100,000+ apps, 70+ ad networks and 200+ billion ad views ([Appodeal eCPM Report 2025](https://appodeal.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Appodeal-The-Latest-eCPM-Report-2025.pdf)), $14–22 US eCPM ([RevenueLab](https://www.revenuelab.fyi/blog/admob-ecpm-benchmarks-2026)) or $15–30 tier-1 ([Playwire](https://www.playwire.com/blog/admob-ecpm-benchmarks-what-publishers-should-expect)), and players preferring rewarded to interstitials four to one ([Unity](https://unity.com/blog/understanding-the-impact-of-rewarded-ads-on-iap-retention-and-engagement)).

Second, the placement Gate Escape already built is the best-performing one in the industry: context-sensitive placements at the moment of need score 38.1% engagement against 23.8% between-level ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/unity-global-rewarded-ad-engagement-rose-by-32-in-2023/)), and "additional moves" is the #2 reward type at 30.5%, within 0.6 points of gacha at 31.1% ([Mistplay](https://business.mistplay.com/resources/rewarded-ads-stats)) — so the ethical exclusion of paid random rewards costs almost nothing in measured engagement.

Third, the retention and spend correlations are large but must be reported honestly: rewarded engagers were 4.5× more likely to make an IAP (9× in two of eight apps), average weighted spend rose 326% and sessions 34%, and D30 retention reached 50%+ against a 13% benchmark ([Unity](https://unity.com/blog/understanding-the-impact-of-rewarded-ads-on-iap-retention-and-engagement)). Every one of those studies compares self-selected engagers against non-engagers, so they are not causal evidence that rewarded ads *cause* engagement. The conclusion that survives is narrower: this is the highest-engagement, highest-completion, lowest-annoyance ad surface available, and Apple explicitly permits it ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

Fourth, paid-upfront is not viable as the primary model. Premium mobile releases rose 77% to just under 750 titles in 2025 and ports grew from 7 to 23 with revenue up 44.6%, but the entire mobile-port revenue pool is roughly $15M a year, top outcomes are IP-backed (Balatro $21.3M, Slay the Spire $13.1M), and the reality check is a 2023 puzzle-platformer port earning "nearly $189,000" total, while 96% of mobile downloads are free-to-play ([GamesIndustry.biz](https://www.gamesindustry.biz/premium-mobile-games-are-back-with-releases-up-77-in-2025)). Combined with the Unblock Me Premium warning — 2.4K ratings against 539K for the free version at a $0.99 gate ([premium](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/unblock-me-premium/id315021242), [free](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/unblock-me/id315019111)) — the Mekorama structure is the correct one.

### 11.2 Ad-load reality, and the one placement to change

A rewarded-only, banner-free, interstitial-free build removes roughly 314 of about 337 lifetime impressions per user relative to a typical puzzle title's 72.5 interstitials, 23.4 rewarded and 241.5 banners ([Appodeal 2025](https://igamingradio.com/press-releases/2025/04/24/140750/appodeals-2025-mobile-casual-benchmarks-report-shows-hybrid-casual-games-significantly-outperforming-hypercasual-when-it-comes-to-ad-based-monetization/)) — about 93% of impression volume gone. Because rewarded eCPMs are the highest of any format, the revenue loss is far smaller than 93%, but ad ARPDAU should be modelled at or below the bottom of the $0.03–$0.10 band ([Juego Studio](https://www.juegostudio.com/blog/arpdau-benchmarks-by-game-genre)). Recommended cadence if forced formats are ever considered: industry guidance starts at 5–7 interstitials and 3–4 rewarded per user per day with interstitials only at natural breaks ([CAS.ai](https://cas.ai/2025/10/09/hybrid-monetization-mobile-games-guide-2-2-2-8-2-2-2-8-3-3-4-5-6-7-3/)), and puzzle practice is every 3–4 levels or after 8–10 minutes ([Yield Solutions](https://www.yieldsolutions.com/blog/match-3-and-puzzle-games-understanding-mobile-ad-revenue-in-high-retention-titles)) — but the recommendation here is to ship no forced formats at all.

The single placement change: **do not let a rewarded rescue silently alter a recorded daily result.** Price it in moves and print the marker, per the two published precedents ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/07/apple-news-plus-introduces-emoji-game/), [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/find-puzzles-in-apple-news-iph4883822da/ios)), and never make an ad the only path to a win — the category's most-quoted principles run the other way: "Puzzmo respects your time and engages your curiosity rather than trying to hook you" ([Editor & Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/puzzmo-launches-mobile-app-exclusively-for-iphone,255847)) and "We're not aiming to keep you glued to our app… We don't want to create an addictive cycle of endless levels" ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2024/01/29/wordle-nyt-games-news-media-layoffs)).

On cosmetics: 46% of regretted purchases were blamed on limited utility or impact and 34% on misaligned advertising of an item's value ([Mistplay 2025](https://business.mistplay.com/reports/mobile-gaming-loyalty-index-2025)) — so paper folios must be previewable before purchase and shown at the moment of desire. The existing ledger and opening-balance/closing-balance rules for any future transaction UI already sit ahead of the EU CPC Key Principles, which require clear real-money pricing, no obscuring of cost via multiple currencies or forced exchanges, no forced purchases, pre-contractual information and a 14-day right of withdrawal ([European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/european-commission-hosts-stakeholders-talks-application-cpc-networks-key-principles-games-virtual-2025-06-03_en), [Arthur Cox](https://www.arthurcox.com/insights/eu-consumer-protection-new-principles-for-virtual-currencies-in-video-games/)).

### Q1 — Closest analogues on each axis, and what their public metrics say

The weekly pick-2-of-4 axis has **no daily-puzzle analogue at all**; its precedents are match-3 (Toy Blast's four switchable missions, Candy Crush's three swaps with visible rewards — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2024/9/23/daily-missions-in-puzzles-why-should-we-see-them-more-often)). Certification cosmetics map onto Royal Match's collection and event cadence, minus the randomness ([Mechanics Playbook](https://gamification.gamebizconsulting.com/case-studies/royal-match/)).

**Posture (no timer, no lives, no randomness):** *Mekorama*, whose top review names it verbatim — "No timers, lives or ridiculous scoring systems" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mekorama/id1079464948)) — and, surprisingly, *Blockudoku*, whose own store copy sells "no time limit", "no rush" at 100M+ downloads and 4.6★ on 1.29M reviews ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.easybrain.block.puzzle.games&hl=en_US)). The posture is therefore not by itself a differentiator; the provable par and the verifiable dark-pattern absence are. Full detail in [§2](#2-where-gate-escape-sits-in-the-market).

### Q2 — Which state-of-the-art features slot in without breaking the guarantees, ranked by expected retention value

1. **A home-screen widget showing today's draft state and the streak** — the largest zero-server retention surface, fully deterministic; half of Duolingo's widget users hold a 6-month streak and retention was "far better" even controlling for prior commitment ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/widget-feature/)).
2. **One opt-in local daily reminder** — the largest documented untouched lever, with the caveat that the 190% figure comes from a vendor study of opt-in users over 90 days ([Airship](https://www.airship.com/newsroom/new-urban-airship-mobile-app-retention-study/)), and the model to copy is LinkedIn's single opt-in reminder ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6269510)).
3. **Endowed-progress framing on the survey and certification bars** — 34% vs 19% redemption from framing alone ([Nunes & Drèze](https://msbfile03.usc.edu/digitalmeasures/jnunes/intellcont/Endowed%20Progress%20Effect-1.pdf)).
4. **A purity token in place of assist counters** — LinkedIn's "Flawless" and Strands' "Solved Without Hints" ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6889085), [NYT Help](https://help.nytimes.com/360011158491-New-York-Times-Games/28214352967700-Strands)).
5. **A contract swap after first progress** — precedented at up to three swaps ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2024/9/23/daily-missions-in-puzzles-why-should-we-see-them-more-often)).
6. **A deterministic par album with tutorial slots pre-filled** — collection drive with zero drops ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/albums-battle-passes-and-milestones-mobiles-top-event-types-to-introduce-this-summer/)).
7. **Branching availability within each sheet (any 8 of 10)** — Hazelden's published mitigation for difficulty a solo dev cannot A/B away ([Rock Paper Shotgun](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/cosmic-express-alan-hazelden)).
8. **An honest "tough one" label on the peak levels** — precedented at Royal Match level 39 ([Mechanics Playbook](https://gamification.gamebizconsulting.com/case-studies/royal-match/)).
9. **A published weekday difficulty curve for the drafts** — removes Connections' core recurring complaint ([Vox](https://www.vox.com/culture/2024/3/26/24111847/connections-nyt-game-hint-word-puzzles)).
10. **An average-player comparator on the draft result card** — CineNerdle's "You vs Avg" pattern, only if the beacon is enabled ([CineNerdle](https://www.cinenerdle.app/)).
11. **Colourblind presets plus custom, control-sensitivity option, and Apple accessibility labels** — five labels are plausibly claimable and become mandatory over time ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels/), [GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/ensure-no-essential-information-is-conveyed-by-a-fixed-colour-alone/)).
12. **A free cosmetic "season sheet"** if a pass-shaped structure is ever wanted — battle passes were in 21% of US iOS top-100 grossing games ([GameRefinery](https://www.gamerefinery.com/battle-pass-trend-mobile-games/)), but only the free cosmetic form fits here.

Explicitly rejected: lives and save-me offers (need timers and IAP), teams and global leaderboards (need servers), paid passes, random collections, and any manufactured near-miss. Detail in [§4](#4-gamification-principles-worth-importing).

### Q3 — Daily-puzzle design: what to adopt and avoid

Adopt: one identical board per day as the load-bearing property ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/12/josh-wardle-interview-wordle/)); first-attempt-is-the-record with labelled unrecorded practice, precedented by the NYT Mini's first-solve timing and Waffle's stats-excluded packs ([NYT Help](https://help.nytimes.com/360011158491-New-York-Times-Games/360025912452-The-Mini-Crossword), [Waffle](https://wafflegame.net/)); a one-axis share string; a purity token; a published day boundary at device-local midnight; the streak-freeze spec already in place; a day→hash manifest that fails loudly; and human pre-testing of all 365 boards for par optimality and human achievability at par+3. Avoid: board fragments in the share string, as LinkedIn does ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6889085)); global leaderboards, chat and accounts, per Puzzmo's own retrospective ([Puzzmo](https://blog.puzzmo.com/posts/2026/02/06/follows-not-friends/)); letting a rescued win read as clean; making the streak the product's identity; and silently changing scoring after launch ([NYT Help](https://help.nytimes.com/360011158491-New-York-Times-Games/24611727334932-Wordle)). On the archive: ship without one — LinkedIn has none and still reports 84% next-day return ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6269510), [LinkedIn news](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/linkedin-announces-zip-)) — and if one is ever added, gate it and make it streak-neutral exactly as all three streak-bearing NYT games do. Full rulings in [§5](#5-the-daily-draft--adoptavoid-rulings).

### Q4 — Choice-driven weekly goals versus fixed daily quests

There is **no head-to-head experiment** in the fetched evidence, and that should be stated plainly rather than papered over. What exists: strong commercial precedent for player-selected missions with free switching in two of the largest match-3 titles ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2024/9/23/daily-missions-in-puzzles-why-should-we-see-them-more-often)); the SDT argument that choice serves autonomy, in a literature a 110-paper review criticises for selective application rather than for being wrong ([Ryan et al.](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf), [Tyack & Mekler](https://pure.itu.dk/ws/files/95235949/Tyack_2020_.pdf)); and evidence that daily quests are mostly experienced positively across 115 games and 178 participants but also generate obligation and a "pest" feeling ([CHI PLAY 2022](https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/files/140472596/3549489.pdf)). Verdict: keep pick-2-of-4, add one swap after first progress, keep the weekly cadence, and treat the retention benefit as judgement. Detail in [§6](#6-the-field-survey-and-weekly-contracts).

### Q5 — Order-constrained clearing: precedents and comprehension limits

Precedents are strong and directly on point: Railbound requires numbered cars to couple in numerical order across 240+ levels and won a 2023 Apple Design Award for interaction; Cosmic Express derives its difficulty from combinatorial explosion in a single line rather than board size; Trainyard versus Freeways establishes binary versus graded judgement as a design choice ([Puzzlebyrinth](https://puzzlebyrinth.com/fr/articles/track-laying-puzzle-design)); and the sort-puzzle family shows dock-slot sequencing tension at commercial scale ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/6/sort-puzzles-how-a-new-subgenre-is-born)). On comprehension limits: **no quantified tolerance threshold exists in the evidence base.** The usable constraints are that the first level using a new mechanic must be easy and explicitly taught ([GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/match-3-games-metrics-guide)), that more than two disclosure layers degrade usability ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)), and that mass-casual titles do add systems late when staggered and signposted ([Mechanics Playbook](https://gamification.gamebizconsulting.com/case-studies/royal-match/)). Sheet 4's current ramp satisfies all three. Detail in [§7](#7-the-approval-chain-and-late-rule-changes).

### Q6 — How the verification stack compares with best-in-class studios

Ahead on correctness and determinism: a completeness oracle, byte-identical regeneration, a differential rule oracle and a fast per-commit gate are things AAA studios spend engineer-years building ([Riot determinism](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/determinism-league-legends-implementation), [Riot automated testing](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/automated-testing-league-legends)). Behind on everything requiring a human or a device: no human playtesters ([GamesUserResearch](https://gamesuserresearch.com/how-many-players-do-i-need-for-a-playtest/)), difficulty measured by an optimal rather than human-like agent ([arXiv:2306.14626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14626)), no crash/ANR instrumentation against Play's published 1.09% and 0.47% thresholds ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2022/10/raising-bar-on-technical-quality-on-google-play.html)), no device matrix ([Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/docs/test-lab)), no staged-rollout plan ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/update-your-app/release-a-version-update-in-phases/)), no remote-config plumbing ([Unity](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/remote-config)), no cohort A/B capability, and no compliance path through Play's 12-tester/14-day closed test ([Play Console](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en)). On A/B specifically: it is overkill, not a gap — even Supercell at ~300M monthly players runs 10–20 experiments per game per quarter ([GrowthBook](https://www.growthbook.io/blog/a-b-testing-300-million-players-without-breaking-their-trust-the-supercell-approach)), and a 7-day minimum cycle at indie traffic is unpowered ([Optimizely](https://support.optimizely.com/hc/en-us/articles/4410283969165-How-long-to-run-an-experiment)). Full table in [§9](#9-the-factory--benchmarked-against-studio-practice-and-ai-qa-state-of-the-art).

### Q7 — State of the art in AI-assisted QA, and which checks are naive by comparison

The frontier has four strands. RL testing agents for coverage, exploit-finding and map difficulty ([EA SEED CoG 2020](https://www.ea.com/seed/news/automated-game-testing-deep-reinforcement-learning)), curiosity-driven agents for states naive exploration cannot reach ([arXiv:2103.13798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.13798)), and the production reality that deploying them is documented as a large time sink even at EA, though it shipped in Battlefield 2042 and Dead Space (2023) ([EA SEED CoG 2023](https://www.ea.com/seed/news/cog23-challenges-deploying-rl-agents-game-testing)). Human-like play-style agents: Ubisoft's CARMI generating player play-styles on unseen levels from summary data alone ([HAL](https://hal.science/hal-03957672v1/document)), King's production bot with 95% fewer manual tweaks ([GamesIndustry.biz](https://www.gamesindustry.biz/how-king-is-using-ai-to-speed-up-development-of-new-candy-crush-levels)), and EA's approach of rebuilding bare-bones mechanics as a separate system so agents "play in minutes what would take testers days", which exposed action imbalances and inconsequential rewards in *The Sims Mobile* and drove shipped design changes ([AAAI AIIDE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/13034)). Difficulty predictors: the best-5%-of-agent-runs predictor ([arXiv:2306.14626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14626)), action-distribution modelling ([CoG 2021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/CoG52621.2021.9619050)), personalized prediction via factorization machines ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3526113.3545624)), and the cohort-plus-simulation fusion result ([arXiv:2401.17436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17436)). LLM GUI agents: AUITestAgent at 94% verification accuracy with 4 new functional bugs found across 10 regression tests over two months in production ([arXiv:2407.09018](https://huggingface.co/papers/2407.09018)), inside a taxonomy that runs random → rule-based → script-based → ML/RL → LLM-brained ([arXiv:2411.18279](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.18279v1)).

Which checks are naive by comparison: **the difficulty sawtooth assertion**, because it is derived from optimal par rather than a human-like distribution; **the absence of any fuzz layer**, when Android's Monkey has offered seeded repeatable stress since 2011 ([Android](https://www.dre.vanderbilt.edu/~schmidt/android/android-4.0/out/target/common/docs/doc-comment-check/guide/developing/tools/monkey.html)); and **single-rater persona severity**, which the usability literature calls untrustworthy ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-rate-the-severity-of-usability-problems/)). What is *not* naive: the solver oracle is strictly stronger than what PCGML-style generation provides, because it gives a hard solvability and par guarantee rather than a learned approximation ([arXiv:1702.00539](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.00539)). Curiosity-driven RL is overkill for a 40-level state space that is already exhaustively solved.

### Q8 — Minimum telemetry model and KPIs for a soft launch, with thresholds

The event catalogue is already sufficient; the property layer is not. The minimum additions are `attempt_no` and `lifetime_attempts_on_level` on every funnel event, a single `result` enum, `moves_used`/`moves_par`/`moves_delta`, `duration_ms`, `ttfi_ms`, `entry_source`, `first_clear`, a `rescue_offer_shown` impression event to give the offer funnel a denominator, `app_background` so session length is computable, the missing `streak_lapse` event, `cohort_day` on session start as the no-user-id workaround, and weekly-goal events, all inside GA4's 500-name / 25-parameter envelope ([GA4 limits](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9267744?hl=en)) with dynamic numerics in value fields ([GameAnalytics](https://docs.gameanalytics.com/events-metrics-and-filtering/event-types/design-events/)) and funnel properties on every step ([Amplitude](https://amplitude.com/docs/data/data-planning-playbook)). Thresholds are tabulated in [§10.4](#104-kill--iterate--scale-thresholds); the headline gates are D1 below 22% kill / 33%+ scale, D7 below 4% kill / 10%+ scale, L1→L2 drop-off above 17% red, mean attempts per level outside 2.0–3.5 for investigation, rewarded engagement below 20% kill / 36%+ scale, and ad ARPDAU below $0.01 kill. Two structural constraints override all of them: every cell needs ~900–1,000 installs ([Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2025-12-16-mobile-soft-launch-complete-guide/)), and at 40 levels this is a D3–D7 test, since 200 levels are needed before D14+ reads ([SayGames](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/how-saygames-uses-game-analytics-to-balance-difficulty-in-hybrid-puzzle-games/)).

### Q9 — Persona reviews: what the note schema is missing

REPRO/EXPECTED/ACTUAL is a sound defect schema. `{area, severity, text}` is not sufficient for a review artefact, on three grounds: single-rater severity is "too unreliable to be trusted" and needs the mean of three independent evaluators ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-rate-the-severity-of-usability-problems/)); severity is a composite of frequency, impact and persistence that should be captured decomposed so it can be recomputed and audited ([same](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-rate-the-severity-of-usability-problems/)); and professional reports carry evidence, causes ordered by impact, an explicit player-impact statement, a positives section because "knowing what not to change can be just as important", thematic grouping ordered most-severe-first, and the method stated in the artefact itself ([Steve Bromley](https://www.stevebromley.com/blog/2017/03/08/whats-in-a-games-user-research-report/)). Add, in priority order: decomposed severity; a second and third independent rater with an inter-rater agreement figure; an evidence artefact (screenshot, video, trace, seed); build/device/OS/locale, mirroring Play's issue detail fields ([Play Console](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844487?hl=en)); causes; player impact; a heuristic reference to the specific Nielsen, PLAY or GAG criterion violated ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-conduct-a-heuristic-evaluation/)); positives; a reproducibility rate as hits over attempts plus the seed; a theme key; and an effort estimate carrying a RICE or MoSCoW label so DO NOW / DESIGN CHANGE / SKIP becomes defensible — with SKIP items **logged**, since "Won't have this time" is recorded specifically to prevent informal reintroduction ([Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/blog/rice-simple-prioritization-for-product-managers/), [Agile Business Consortium](https://www.agilebusiness.org/resource/what-is-moscow-prioritization/)). One process note worth borrowing: Player Research deliberately does not score games against a fixed heuristic set, preferring psychological principles contextualised by the team's stated design intent ([Player Research](https://www.playerresearch.com/learn/games-user-research-methodology-series-usability-expert-analysis/)) — so the heuristic reference should be a tag, not the rubric.

### Q10 — What to change about the iteration order, and the single most important next measurement

The current order — research → rules → passes → critic → developer pass — has one structural flaw: **every verification step is synthetic, and the loop closes before any human enters it.** Three changes, in order.

First, insert a human step between passes and critic, not after it. The reason is not sequencing aesthetics: expert heuristic review and user testing were found to identify *qualitatively different* issues and to be complements rather than substitutes ([Desurvire et al.](https://www.valuesatplay.org/wp-content/uploads/2007/09/desurvireplayabilityheurist.pdf)), and running the critic first means the critic's 20 notes set the developer pass's agenda before any real comprehension data exists. Six unmoderated sessions at about $69 each, delivered in roughly a day, would do it ([PlaytestCloud](https://www.playtestcloud.com/single-session-playtest), [round-up](https://gamesuserresearch.com/top-remote-playtest-platforms-for-unmoderated-testing/)).

Second, split the critic into three parallel independent raters as the default rather than the option, and average severity — the capability already exists and the standard is three to five ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-conduct-a-heuristic-evaluation/)).

Third, make the research → rules step bidirectional. Bright-line rules in the instructions file are excellent for preventing regression, but they also freeze positions taken on thin evidence; the rules file should carry an evidence-strength tag per rule so that a rule resting on a studio blog can be revisited when local data contradicts it, while a rule resting on a controlled experiment cannot. One further borrowing: Duolingo's product lesson was to optimise the widest step of the funnel, prioritising a 1% gain at the initial-invite step over power-user gains ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/product-lessons-friend-streak/)) — for Gate Escape the widest step is level 1 completion, not sheet completion, so the round's agenda should be weighted there.

**The single most important measurement for the next cycle: attempts-to-first-clear per level, from real humans, on levels 1 through 21.** It is the operationalisation of difficulty in the literature ([arXiv 2401.17436](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17436v1)), it is the input the pass-rate-versus-quit-rate heuristic needs ([Ludaxis](https://www.ludaxis.io/blog/level-design-puzzle-games-guide)), it is the only way to test whether the asserted sawtooth is a human sawtooth, it is readable from six playtest sessions before any store release, and it is the one number that determines whether the exam at L20 is the intended peak or an unintended wall. Second priority, and cheap to obtain simultaneously: time-to-first-input on L31, the approval chain's teaching level, as the comprehension signal for the one late rule change in the game.
