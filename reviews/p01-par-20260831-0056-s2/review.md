# Gate Escape — mid-game review (levels 11–20)
**Juno Adler · iPhone 17 Simulator · 10/10 cleared, 9 three-star, 1 rescue**

## Verdict — 6.5/10

Two games stapled together. Levels 11–15 are a to-do list: par equals the block count exactly, so every piece leaves in one drag and the only question is what order to do the obvious in. I cleared four at par without touching undo. Then 16 brings a real deadlock, 17 a fourth colour and the first forced double-move, and 19 and 20 are properly good — the kind where a block walks to the wrong end of the board and comes back. The craft underneath is better than the curve on top of it.

*Played via a scripted console — I can't judge finger feel, latency or audio.*

## What is genuinely good

**The one-drag rule.** A whole route costs one move: on L20 a green L went right along the ceiling, down the far wall, back left and out the floor in one gesture. In fifty-plus drags the router never once picked a path I didn't intend.

**The fail card.** "So close! 5 of 6 blocks escaped — the last block is one drag from its gate", with the stranded piece highlighted and a ghost route drawn to its exit. Textbook near-miss framing, and it made me want the three moves.

**Wordless feedback.** The top gate desaturated the instant the last red circle left. Same thinking in the shape stamps and crosshatched stones: three seconds, sound off, I read every board here.

**Undo refunds the move** — 3 back to 2 — which makes a par+3 cap a puzzle rather than a tax. Nothing tells the player so.

## Top improvements, ranked

1. **Add a hint button.** Nowhere — not HUD, not pause, not the fail card — can a stuck player ask for help. It's the genre's highest-yield rewarded-video slot and its main churn valve; without it a player stuck at L25 brute-forces or leaves, and you learn nothing. Put it beside undo, ad-gated after two free.
2. **Make par exceed block count from L12.** L11–15 all ran 6 blocks / par 6 / limit 9. This is where session length and D3 are won, and a player never made to think has nothing to be proud of. Build around one block that must park and be retrieved, keeping the cap at par+3.
3. **Build out the win card.** Three stars, one line, one button: the most-viewed screen in the game, celebrating and converting nothing. Add a star total ticking up, a chapter bar toward a chest, a coin drop and a rewarded-video double-up — Next level still the default.
4. **Separate adjacent same-colour blocks.** The two red L-trominoes atop L16 read as one mass; I needed 3× zoom to find the seam. That breaks your own 3-second rule, and same-colour pairs only get commoner. Inset each block so a dark gutter always shows.
5. **Split L14's four new ideas across three levels.** Three L-trominoes *and* a 2×2 on the densest board yet. The rule exists because the level after the confusing one is the one they don't open. One L on a sparse board at 14, a second at 15, the square alone at 16.

Smaller: the fail card covers the gate its own route points at, and the HUD never says how many blocks remain.

## Curve, level by level

- **11** — Par 6 for 6 blocks; every piece exits in a straight line. Too soft for L11.
- **12** — Gates on three walls, the only new idea. Cleared cold.
- **13** — Same arithmetic a third time; board 80% empty by move three.
- **14** — L-shapes *and* a 2×2 together: four new ideas at once.
- **15** — Five L-shapes, densest board yet; legibility holds. Nothing moves twice.
- **16** — First real deadlock: green domino and red L each need the other gone.
- **17** — Fourth colour, 7×9 board, par 8 for 7 blocks. First forced double-move. Excellent.
- **18** — Second mutual dependency; a cyan pair parks in the far corner. Good.
- **19** — Two gates on one wall; cyan sacrifices itself to the opposite corner. Best here.
- **20** — Spike comes from tightening the cap to par+2, not deepening the board.

Every level, the good ones included, peaks at move one and deflates: pieces only leave, nothing replaces them, so each ends on its dullest beat.

**Fail/rescue & monetization.** The rescue is strong, honest, and the *only* commercial surface in the game. Its button reads "AD | +3 moves · free rescue" — paid and free in one breath. Pick one contract per state.

**Retention.** "Replay for ★★★" is the single hook, and a good one. Otherwise no chapters, chest, streak or daily; the map shows all thirty tiles in one flat grid — a demo, not a product.

## Originality

The one-drag-any-route rule and the gate *lane bands* — a block must land on specific rows or columns before it can leave — are two real ideas Color Block Jam doesn't have. The lane band is the better one and it's underused: on L17 and L19 it made me plan a landing position, not a direction — a second axis of difficulty the leaders don't offer. A publisher picks this up if the front half plays like L19 and the meta exists. The mechanic is ready; the game around it isn't.


## Improvement notes (as they happened)

- **t3 · L11 · minor · retention** — The level map shows all 30 levels as one flat grid with no chapter/world breaks, no themed sections, and no end-of-chapter reward beat. Players can see the entire game from the map screen, which reads as 'demo' and removes any mid-run goal to chase; hybrid-casual retention leans hard on 'clear the chapter, get the chest' loops (D1/D7 both track to visible near-term goals). I'd group levels into worlds of 10 with a header, a progress bar toward the next chest, and lock future chapters behind a card so the map implies more game beyond what's drawn.
- **t7 · L11 · minor · ui** — There is a large empty band (roughly the top third of the play area) between the HUD row and the top of the grid, and the HUD never states how many blocks remain — only moves left, stars and par. Blocks-remaining is the cheapest tension meter in the genre and the one number players watch on a dense board; without it the endgame of a level reads flat, and the wasted band makes the screen look like an unfinished layout. I'd float a compact objective row in that gap: one chip per gate colour showing blocks still to clear (e.g. red circle x2, cyan triangle x1), ticking down as they escape.
- **t10 · L11 · major · monetization** — The win card is three stars, one line of text and a Next level button — no running star total, no chapter progress bar, no 'beat par by 2' flourish, no coin/booster reward, no streak. This is the most-viewed screen in the game and in hybrid-casual it is where the meta and the ad/IAP surface live (rewarded-video 'double your coins' on the win card is the single highest-yield placement in the genre, and progress-toward-chest here is what drives the next-session return). As it stands the card converts nothing and celebrates nothing. I'd add: star total ticking up, a chapter progress bar filling with a chest at the end, a coin drop, and a rewarded-video double-up button — and keep Next level as the big obvious default so the flow stays fast.
- **t17 · L12 · major · difficulty** — On levels 11 and 12, par equals the number of blocks exactly (6 blocks, par 6), so every piece exits in one uninterrupted drag and the only decision is sequencing. Two levels into the mid-game I cleared both at par with no undo and no wrong move, which means the difficulty curve has flattened right where a hybrid-casual player should start feeling resistance (L11-15 is where session length and D3 retention are won or lost — a player who is never made to think has no reason to be proud of clearing 12). The fix is not a tighter move cap; it is levels where at least one block must be parked out of the way and retrieved later, so par exceeds block count. Make 'a block has to move twice' arrive by L13 and recur, and let the move limit stay par+3 so the tension comes from the puzzle, not the budget.
- **t19 · L13 · critical · monetization** — There is no hint affordance anywhere — not in the HUD, not in pause, not on the fail card. A stuck player's only options are restart or quit. In hybrid-casual, the hint button is simultaneously the primary rewarded-video placement (watch an ad, reveal the next move) and the main churn valve on hard levels; without it, every player who gets genuinely stuck at L20 or L25 either brute-forces or leaves, and there is no data on where they struggle. I'd add a hint button in the HUD next to undo: first two hints per level free while the tutorial curve runs, then ad-gated or coin-priced, showing exactly one next move (highlight the block and ghost its route). It doubles as the telemetry that tells you which levels are miscalibrated.
- **t23 · L13 · major · difficulty** — Every level so far peaks in difficulty at move one and gets easier with each escape, because blocks only ever leave the board and nothing replaces them. By the halfway point of L11-13 I was looking at a near-empty grid with two or three isolated pieces and no decisions left, so each level ends on its dullest moment instead of its tensest. Compare Color Block Jam, where the board stays under pressure to the last piece. Two fixes worth prototyping: (a) design the back half of the level so the final pieces are the tangled ones — put the interlocked cluster on the pieces whose gates unlock last; (b) introduce a mechanic that adds pressure as the board empties, e.g. a gate that closes after N moves, or a block that must be escorted out last. Ending each level on its flattest beat is why the perfect clears feel unearned.
- **t27 · L14 · major · onboarding** — L14 introduces L-trominoes and a 2x2 square simultaneously — three separate L pieces plus the square land on the same board, which is also the densest so far. The house rule is one new obstacle at a time, and this breaks it at exactly the moment the mid-game should be teaching: a player has to work out that an L needs every one of its columns inside the gate lanes, and that a 2x2 needs two lanes, in the same puzzle. That is where mid-funnel drop-off comes from — the level after the one that confused them is the one they don't open. I'd split it: L14 gets exactly one L-shaped block on an otherwise sparse, forgiving board so the shape rule is discovered safely; L15 adds a second L; the 2x2 square waits until L16 and arrives alone. Then let them combine from L17.
- **t28 · L14 · minor · legibility** — The colour-identity symbol (circle/triangle/diamond) is drawn at a fixed pixel size regardless of block area, so on the 2x2 square at L14 it is a small dot floating in a large field while on a single cell it nearly fills the tile. That inverts the intent: the biggest, most board-dominating pieces get the weakest shape cue, which hurts exactly the colour-blind and glance-play cases the shape cue exists for. There is also a faint pale square artifact showing inside the circle on the 2x2. I'd scale the glyph with the block's shorter dimension (or repeat it once per 2 cells) and check the artifact.
- **t38 · L15 · minor · feedback** — Undo refunds the spent move as well as reverting the position, which makes the tight par+3 limit fair rather than punitive — but this is never communicated. Nothing in the how-to-play, the HUD, or the first use of undo says the move comes back, so cautious players will avoid experimenting and treat every drag as irreversible; that is the behaviour that makes a move-limited puzzle feel stressful instead of thoughtful. I'd surface it once: the first time a player crosses par on any level, flash a one-line coach tip on the undo button ('Undo is free - it gives the move back too'), and put the same line in How to play.
- **t41 · L15 · minor · feedback** — The HUD star meter reflects moves spent so far against par, not the best rating still achievable. Five moves in on L15 with five blocks left and four moves remaining, it still showed three gold stars even though three stars was already impossible. Players read that meter as their live score, so it delivers the bad news only at the moment of failure — which removes the tension that a tightening rating is supposed to create and makes the drop feel arbitrary. I'd compute the meter as 'best rating still reachable' (moves used plus the minimum remaining escapes) so it dims a star the instant that star is gone, and give it a small shudder when it drops.
- **t44 · L15 · nit · feedback** — RETRACTION of my earlier star-meter note: on zooming the rendered HUD, the star meter IS live — at 7 moves used against par 6 it had already dropped to one gold star and the move counter had turned red. My earlier claim that it only reports spent moves was read off a text readout, not the screen, and is wrong. The meter works. The one thing still worth a look is the band width: going from three stars at par straight to one star at par+1 is a steep cliff, and if that is the intent it should at least be stated on the how-to-play screen so players know what a star costs.
- **t46 · L15 · minor · monetization** — The fail card is the strongest screen in the build - 'So close! 5 of 6 blocks escaped', with the stranded block highlighted and a dashed ghost route drawn to its gate - but two things undercut it. (1) The card is anchored to the bottom of the screen and covers the lower rows of the board, so on L15 the ghost route ran straight underneath the card and the destination gate was hidden; the player cannot verify the promise the card is making. Anchor the card to whichever half of the board is empty, or shrink and raise it so the highlighted block and its gate stay visible. (2) The button reads 'AD | +3 moves - free rescue', which says paid and free simultaneously; players learn to distrust that. Pick one contract per state: 'Watch ad for +3 moves' when it costs a view, 'Free rescue - +3 moves' for the once-per-level freebie, and consider making the first rescue of a session free and ad-gating the rest.
- **t49 · L15 · nit · ui** — On a sub-3-star win the card reads 'Solved in 10 moves (best: 6)' - but 6 is par, not the player's own best, and 'best' overwhelmingly reads as a personal record. On a level a player has genuinely beaten before this becomes actively wrong. Say 'Solved in 10 moves - par 6' and, once a personal best exists, add it as a separate line ('your best: 8'). The 'Replay for three stars' button underneath is a strong hook and deserves clearer numbers next to it.
- **t50 · L16 · major · legibility** — When two blocks of the same colour sit edge to edge - the two red L-trominoes at the top of L16 - they merge into a single visual mass. The only separator is a hairline double outline plus small corner dots, which at real phone size is two or three pixels and disappears at a glance or at arm's length; I misread the cluster as one piece until I zoomed 3x. This breaks the 3-second sound-off legibility rule directly, and it gets worse from here, because block counts and same-colour pairs both rise through the twenties. Fixes, cheapest first: inset every block by a pixel or two so a dark gutter always separates neighbours; add a subtle drop shadow on each block so edges read as separate objects; or alternate the hatch angle between adjacent blocks of the same colour. The corner dots already there are a good idea - make them bigger and brighter and they'd carry more of the load.
- **t92 · L20 · minor · difficulty** — L20's spike is delivered by shrinking the move buffer from par+3 to par+2 rather than by deepening the puzzle. The board is genuinely knotted - the green L walled in by a stone on one side and the amber square on the other is the tightest position in the range - so the tighter cap is not needed to make it hard, and a smaller buffer mostly punishes exploration rather than rewarding insight (players experiment less exactly when the level most wants them to). Keep the cap at par+3 through L20 and let the puzzle carry the spike; if you want a visible escalation, spike the piece count and the number of forced double-moves instead, and save the tighter buffer for a dedicated 'expert' variant.
