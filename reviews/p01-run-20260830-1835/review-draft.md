# Gate Escape — first-play review (Juno Adler)

Played 10 minutes on iPhone 17: menu, How to play, levels 1–10 (nine at par, one deliberate fail on L6 to test the rescue), pause menu, level select.

## 1. Verdict — 7/10

Gate Escape is the cleanest-reading unblock prototype I have seen this year, and it has one honest idea the genre leaders lack: a drag is a move, however far it travels and however many corners it turns. That rule turns Color Block Jam's tap-tap-tap into route planning, and it feels slick every time a bar slides up a column, hooks right and vanishes. Legibility is exemplary — every gate is a colored strip with a shape, every block carries the same shape, and I never needed sound. What holds it back is everything around the board: a flat win beat, a rescue card that hides what it is selling, a move budget so generous the monetization surface never appears naturally, and no undo. Those are polish problems, not design problems, and a sprint fixes them.

## 2. What is genuinely good

- **3-second legibility, every board.** L1 is one red block, one red gate, one white arrow. L6's stacked cyan/red gates on one wall read instantly because color and shape are doubled. L8's third color (green diamond) caused no confusion.
- **The corner-drag rule is a real hook.** L3's first long bar (left, corner, down, out) is where the game clicks; L9's wide green climbing a whole column and hooking right is the payoff.
- **Textbook curve through L5.** L1–2 cannot be failed, L3 teaches corners, L4 introduces ordering, L7 needs a real read, L10 is the first board where nothing can escape on move one and par exceeds block count. One idea at a time.
- **Controls.** Blocks snap to cells and never fight the finger. Zero mis-drags in 70 turns.
- **Retention scaffolding exists.** Level select shows stars per tile, hatched locked tiles, gold ring on the current one, "6 of 30 cleared · 16 stars." My lone one star on L06 sat there taunting me — the loop works.
- **Pause menu** is complete and correctly ordered.

## 3. Top improvements, ranked

1. **Make the win beat land.** *Saw:* three stars already lit, static, no particles, Next tappable instantly, identical card every time. *Why:* the win screen drives session length; flat cards make ten perfects feel like spreadsheets. *Change:* staggered star pops, a burst on the third, short delay before Next, fix "1 moves."
2. **Tighten the move budget.** *Saw:* limit = par + 6 on every level. *Why:* a player who never learns corners still clears L1–10, so no tension and no rescue impressions before L10+. *Change:* par + 2 or + 3 from L5, star thresholds visible in the HUD.
3. **Show what the rescue is buying.** *Saw:* "So close! 4 of 5" with a green +3 over a blurred board; no price, ad icon or coin balance. *Why:* the one block a single drag from freedom is the whole pitch and it is hidden. *Change:* keep the board sharp, pulse the remaining block, ghost the route, label the offer.
4. **Add undo.** *Saw:* none; a misdrag costs a move with restart as the only recourse. *Why:* prevents rage-quits at the moment the fail card fires for the wrong reason. *Change:* one-step undo on the HUD.
5. **Replay on sub-3-star wins.** *Saw:* one-star card ("12 moves, best: 5") offers only Next. *Change:* "Replay for ★★★" button; cheapest retention loop in the genre.
6. **Warn before the cliff.** Counter turns red only at 1 move left. Amber at par, red when movesLeft ≤ blocksLeft.
7. **Teach the corner rule in-board** on L3; How to play is opt-in.
8. **Dim dead gates** once a color is cleared.
9. Smaller: early boards mostly empty (shrink to 4×5); pause blurs the board (dim instead); "Reset progress" is a bare one-tap link; green is "square" in text but a diamond on screen; stones never appeared in 10 levels.

## 4. Fail/rescue, difficulty, retention

**Fail/rescue.** Structurally right: honest copy, green primary CTA, Retry secondary, +3 once per level; the follow-up one-star card is honest (best: 5). But it only appeared because I burned seven moves on purpose. A real player will not see it before L10+, and the blurred board and unlabeled offer will under-convert when they do. Fix the budget and the card together.

**Difficulty.** Well shaped but gentle: a veteran clears L1–10 at par on sight, and the advertised stone obstacle never showed. Bring stones in by L5 and the first "looks free but must wait" trap by L7; keep the L20–25 spike.

**Retention.** Stars, totals, level select and persistence all work. Missing: replay on low-star wins, celebration on the win card, any reason to revisit the menu (no daily, streak or cosmetic). The scaffolding is there; the reasons to climb it are not.

## 5. Originality — why a publisher picks this

Against Color Block Jam and its clones, the differentiator is the drag-as-route move economy: par and stars are computed on whole routes, so skill is seeing the path, not counting taps. That is a marketable mechanic ("one drag, around the corner, out") that demos in a five-second ad. The colorblind-safe shape-on-gate system and blueprint art also stand out on a store page of candy plastic. To win the meeting: tighten the budget so the route rule matters from L5, put juice on the win and fail beats, and show a stone-based twist by L5 so the pitch is "route planning around obstacles." The bones are strong; the surface needs a polish sprint.
