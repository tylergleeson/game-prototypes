GATE ESCAPE — COMPLETE DESIGN & PLAY BRIEF
===========================================
Prepared 2026-09-02 for external research review.
Purpose: (1) position this game against currently trending mobile puzzle
games — where it is derivative, where it differs, and whether the
differences matter commercially; (2) identify additional gamification
opportunities consistent with the constraints in section 10. Specific
questions for the researcher are in section 13.

Current state: fully playable; 30 machine-verified levels; native iOS build
(not yet on the App Store); shareable web build; no monetization active.


1. ONE-PARAGRAPH OVERVIEW
-------------------------
Gate Escape is a single-finger, grid-based "block escape" puzzle in the
hybrid-casual family of Color Block Jam. The board is drawn as an
architect's blueprint. Colored blocks of various shapes sit on a grid;
each edge of the board carries colored "gates." The player drags blocks —
one drag may travel any distance, including around corners — and a block
leaves the board when it reaches a wall gate of its own color that spans
every row/column the block occupies. Clear all blocks within a move limit
to win. Every level is generated with a solver that proves solvability and
computes optimal par; nothing is hand-tuned or adjusted at runtime.


2. CORE RULES (EXACT)
---------------------
Board: rectangular grid, from 4x5 (level 1) up to 7x9 (late levels).
Cells may contain: a block cell, a stone (immovable 1x1 obstacle), or
nothing.

Blocks: polyomino shapes — 1x1, 1x2, 1x3 (both orientations), 2x2 squares,
and L-triominoes. Each block has one of up to 4 colors. Color is never the
only signal: every color carries a distinct printed symbol (circle,
triangle, diamond, dot) on the block AND on its gates (colorblind-safe by
construction).

Gates: colored openings along the outer walls, each spanning a contiguous
run of 1-4 lanes on one side. A block may exit only through a gate of its
own color, only when flush against that wall, and only when the gate
covers EVERY lane (row or column) the block occupies. There is no partial
or misaligned exit; the rule is checked cell-exactly.

Movement: drag with one finger. A drag routes the block cell-by-cell
through free space, corners allowed, following the finger — the block
never passes through blocks, stones, or walls. ONE DRAG = ONE MOVE, no
matter how far it travels or how many corners it turns. Releasing ends the
move. Reaching a valid gate mid-drag exits the block (also one move).
An undo button reverses the last move.

Move economy: each level has "moves" (the limit) and "par" (solver-proven
minimum drags). Limits run 5-12; par runs 1-9. Stars on completion:
3 stars at or under par, 2 stars within par+2, 1 star otherwise. A live
star meter shows the current attainable stars during play.

Failure and rescue: running out of moves with blocks remaining raises a
fail sheet at the moment of loss showing the board state and offering
RESCUE: +3 moves, granted through a (currently free, simulated) rewarded-
ad flow, once per level attempt. Declining offers retry. This fail/rescue
moment is deliberately the future monetization surface.

Hints: an in-engine A* solver can show a "designer's reference" ghost
route for one block — the exact drag path drawn on the blueprint — also
behind the simulated rewarded-ad flow.

Near-miss integrity rule (hard constraint): near misses are STATE TRUTH
only. The game never manipulates a board, deal, or outcome post-hoc to
manufacture "so close!" moments. Deterministic levels are the product.


3. CONTROLS & GAME FEEL
-----------------------
- Single verb: drag. No taps-to-select, no buttons during play.
- Feel beats: a 70 ms press dip on pickup; per-cell haptic ticks as the
  block walks under the finger (rate-limited); a damped snap on release.
- Exit choreography (recently reworked for legibility): the rendered block
  visibly walks the exact cell path the logic took (34 ms/cell, catch-up
  bounded at 260 ms), the gate's lanes flash the instant the block aligns,
  a 90 ms held beat, then the block flies out with a particle burst and a
  rising pitch. Consecutive quick exits climb in pitch (resets after ~4 s)
  — a combo feel without any score mechanic.
- Sound is layered but optional; the game is fully legible with sound off
  (a hard design rule: any screenshot must be readable in 3 seconds).
- Reduced-motion accessibility: every animation shortens or halves
  (particles, shake, glide) under the system setting or an in-game toggle.
- Haptics: native driver on iOS (UIKit generators + one Core Haptics
  "whoosh" signature), with an independent toggle.


4. LEVEL DESIGN & DIFFICULTY CURVE
----------------------------------
All 40 levels are produced by a generator + solver toolchain:
- The generator places blocks/stones/gates per a difficulty spec; the
  solver (breadth-first over full board states) proves solvability and
  computes par. Unsolvable or degenerate boards are discarded.
- Per-level seeds: retuning one level leaves all others byte-identical.
- A headless bot plays every level to par through the real engine before
  any change ships (plus ~70 regression checks for UI flows).

The curve follows the hybrid-casual "CrazyLabs" discipline:
- L1-2: no-fail tutorials (one block, then two; generous limits; L1-3 show
  a ghost-route tutorial hint automatically).
- One new element at a time: longer blocks and a second color by L4;
  stones (immovable obstacles) first appear at L5; a third color ~L8;
  L-shaped and 2x2 blocks mid-teens; a fourth color and 7x9 boards late.
- Tension between par and limit narrows with progress (early levels allow
  par+4; later levels par+2 to par+3).
- A deliberate difficulty spike in the L20-25 band (the genre-standard
  "commitment test"), then breathing room.
- Levels are grouped into "sheets" (chapters) of 10 in the level select:
  Foundations, Corked, The spike, and Sign-off.
- SHEET 4 (L31-40) is the first mechanic extension: the APPROVAL CHAIN.
  Some blocks carry a revision-stamp number and must leave in that order (a
  numbered block may exit only while its number is the lowest still on the
  board); unchained blocks are never gated and movement is never gated at
  all. The generator solves every chained board TWICE - bare and numbered -
  and ships it only if the numbers are worth a stated minimum of extra
  drags, so a chain is never decoration. L31 is the one exception: it costs
  nothing and exists to teach the rule. Chains are capped at 4 because the
  in-game hint solver has to keep answering, and the generator measures
  exactly what the hint will cost before shipping a board.


5. VISUAL IDENTITY
------------------
The entire game is an architect's blueprint: cyanotype-blue paper, white
line work, a drafting title block as the menu, the how-to-play as the
drawing's "legend," level select as a "sheet index" with a certification
stamp per sheet.
Blocks are cross-hatched solids with corner registration marks; gates are
tabs on the wall with matching hatch + symbol; stones are cross-hatched
voids. Everything the player can act on has solid fill plus outline
(a hard rule). Unlockable "paper" skins re-ink the whole drawing (Sepia,
Night vellum, Whiteprint) without touching layout or legibility.

Cold open (recently reworked): a calm cover sheet — title treatment, one
status stamp line (LEVEL 12 / 40 · star count · streak), and exactly three
actions: Play/Continue, Levels, How to play. Fresh installs see a small
vignette of a red block gliding out through its gate (teaching the core
verb before the first tap). All meta surfaces sit one tap deeper.


6. SESSION FLOW
---------------
Cold open -> cover sheet -> Continue (one tap) -> play.
A level takes 20-90 seconds. Win card: stars with a burst, move count vs
par, a survey stamp when one lands (contract filed / week sealed / new
best streak), Next / Replay-for-3-stars. Fail
sheet: rescue offer at the moment of loss. Pause: resume/restart/exit plus
sound, haptics, and motion toggles. The legend (how-to-play) includes an
"around the game" section documenting the meta systems, and animated
demonstrations of the drag and exit rules including the one-drag-corners
rule.


7. META & RETENTION SYSTEMS (ALL SHIPPED)
-----------------------------------------
(Updated 2026-09-02 after the research round. Two things changed: lives are
now OFF by default, and the streak-repair ad was deleted outright. Both are
noted in place below.)

Stars & sheet certification: stars accumulate per sheet of 10 levels; at 24
stars the sheet is CERTIFIED and grants a cosmetic paper skin. Certification
is purely cosmetic, fully deterministic (no randomness), and previewable.
The sheet header carries a stamp glyph — a dashed pending frame while short,
a solid stamped frame with a star once earned, so the state reads by shape
and not by colour alone. (This mechanic shipped as a treasure "chest"; only
the language and the glyph changed on 2026-09-02, never the 24-star rule.)

Daily Draft: one board a day, the SAME board for every player, added on
2026-09-02. It is precomputed — a year of solver-verified boards ships as a
compact table and nothing about a board is generated on the device, because a
generator on the device is a solver on the device and par has to be a fact the
player cannot dial. The draft lives OUTSIDE the campaign entirely: it never
stars a level, never moves the unlock or resume pointer, never certifies a
sheet, never writes a personal best and never costs a life. The weekday curve
rises to a Saturday peak; the move limit is par+3 every day.

  * The sheet index carries a "Daily draft - <date>" row. It reads READY while
    today's record is open and a tap loads the board; the HUD and the pause
    card name the draft by date, never by a level number.
  * The FIRST attempt is the one that is recorded, and the record closes on
    the first RESOLUTION: a clear, or a loss the player resolves by declining
    the rescue (retrying, leaving, closing the page). The fail sheet alone
    decides nothing, because taking the rescue keeps the same attempt alive —
    and a rescue that leads to a clear is recorded as a fact, never hidden.
  * Once the day has closed the row states the result (three stars and FILED,
    or NOT CLEARED) with "practice - not recorded" under it; a tap then opens
    the FIELD REPORT card instead of the board. Everything after that is
    practice and rewrites nothing.
  * The FIELD REPORT is five short lines — a par bar, stars, moves against
    par, route efficiency, undo/hint counts, and "rescued" when one was taken.
    It contains NO route and NO per-move grid, deliberately: every player is
    on the same board, so a picture of the line would be a walkthrough. Two
    different boards played to the same numbers produce the same report but
    for the date, which is the proof that nothing about the board leaks. The
    card shows that exact string in a block above the Share button — what you
    send is what you see — and sharing tries the OS share sheet, then the
    clipboard, then hands the text over in a selectable box.
  * Nothing about the draft is ever sold, and a skipped day is a day that went
    by, not a debt: the row never counts down and never asks.

Staged disclosure (the FTUE ladder, 2026-09-02): a new save opens BARE. The
sheet index shows the level, the stars, the forty tiles and Sound, and
nothing else — no certification stamps, no paper picker, no draft row, no
survey row, and no status line on the landing. Each system arrives on the win
that makes it mean something: certification (with the paper picker) after two
levels cleared, the Daily Draft after three, the Field Survey after five. Each
arrival is announced by ONE quiet stamped NEW row on the win card — the same
row the survey beats use, so the tutorial adds no surface of its own — and it
is shown once, ever. The survey is revealed with the EASIEST of the week's
four contracts already taken, as a worked example rather than a demand for two
decisions about a system the player has never seen; swapping stays free until
progress, exactly as if they had picked it. From the first RETURN day the
landing gains a passive status line — a div, never a button, because the
landing is exactly three taps and that is not negotiable — carrying at most
two finished facts ("Today's draft is filed - 3 of 7 survey days"). It may
never carry a countdown, a call to action, or a statement of something lost.
The first time a player runs out of moves the fail sheet gains one calm line
naming what the rescue and Retry actually do, also once ever. The whole ladder
is DERIVED from cleared levels; only two things are stored, because neither
can be derived — the date of the first clear and which reveals have played.

Field Survey: ONE weekly sheet, and the only meta system besides
certification. As of 2026-09-02 the three daily quests, the streak card and
the weekly ladder were MERGED into it — the research round's verdict was that
three overlapping systems asked the player to track three clocks for one
behaviour. The sheet index now carries a single row (FIELD SURVEY - n/7 -
N pts, with a SELECT 2 badge while the week's contracts are unchosen) that
opens the week's sheet. On it:

  * A seven-day spine, Monday to Sunday. Any level clear stamps today, once.
    The four states read apart by glyph, never by colour: a check for a
    stamped day, a tilde for a weather delay, an open circle for a day that
    went by, a dot for a day still to come.
  * Two CONTRACTS, chosen from the four the week offers. The four are seeded
    deterministically from the ISO week, so every player is offered the same
    four; they come from the same safe telemetry templates the daily quests
    used (clear N levels, earn N stars, N at par, no undos, no hints, N
    blocks), retargeted to a week. Swapping is free until a chosen contract
    earns its first progress; after that the pair is set for the week. That
    is the deliberate design bet of the merge: a choice you can undo forever
    is not a choice, and one you can never revisit punishes a blind first tap.
  * The point marks at 3 / 7 / 12 / 20 — the old ladder's rule, unchanged:
    1 point per clear, +1 at par. No leaderboard, no social comparison; it is
    self-competition only, and everyone can finish.
  * The weekly seal. Filing one contract banks a WEATHER DELAY (max 2 held —
    the old streak freeze, renamed); filing both seals the week and yields a
    fragment, a keepsake tally with nothing gated on it.

Streak: unchanged, and deliberately so — the merge kept its saved state
byte-identical so no real streak was lost. Consecutive calendar days with at
least one clear; its fact now sits in the survey sheet's header rather than
on a row of its own. Missing a day consumes a banked weather delay before
breaking, and that day is stamped on the spine as a delay. With nothing
banked the streak LAPSES SILENTLY — as of 2026-09-02 there is no repair
surface of any kind: no card, no ad, no offer at the moment of loss. The
counter simply clears and the next clear starts a new streak at 1. The open
question is no longer whether to sell the repair but whether the silent reset
READS as calm rather than as something quietly going missing; the next critic
session is scoped to answer that, and to say whether "choose 2 of 4, set once
you start" reads as a real decision or as a trap.

Lives: OFF BY DEFAULT as of 2026-09-02 (the research round's answer to Q6).
The shipped game has no energy gate at all — no hearts anywhere, and a
failed level can be retried forever. The system is not deleted, only
defaulted off: `?lives=1` (or `ge_flags {"lives":1}`, or `GE.livesEnabled`)
restores the whole economy, which the playtest bot still exercises end to
end so the code cannot rot. When on: 5 lives; losing a level from L6 onward
costs one (L1-5 are free; rescue preserves the life); refill one life per 25
minutes, or a rewarded-ad refill.

Hints & undo: as above; undo is unlimited and free (the puzzle is the
product; input error should never be punished).


8. MONETIZATION STANCE (CURRENT: NONE ACTIVE)
---------------------------------------------
Nothing is for sale and no real ads run. The design reserves:
- Rewarded-ad placements: rescue (+3 moves), hint, and — only if lives are
  ever turned back on — the life refill. The streak-repair placement was
  removed on 2026-09-02: selling a player their streak back at the moment
  they lost it is exactly the pressure this product does not want. All
  remaining slots are simulated with a ~3 s placeholder countdown.
- Future IAP candidates: cosmetic papers, a one-time "remove ads."
Hard rules already encoded for whenever money ships:
- NEVER celebratory feedback (confetti/chimes/flashes) on a purchase
  event (the celebration-on-spend pattern is prohibited).
- Every currency grant/spend gets an immutable ledger entry; any
  transaction UI shows opening balance, cost, reward, closing balance.
- No "loss disguised as win" — win language never decorates a net-negative
  outcome.
- Paid random rewards (loot boxes, paid gacha) are permanently off the
  roadmap; fixed bundles only.


9. TECHNICAL PROPERTIES THAT SHAPE DESIGN
-----------------------------------------
- Zero-dependency web engine (canvas) wrapped in a native iOS shell;
  levels are static data shipped with the build.
- Determinism end-to-end: same level plays identically forever; there is
  no server, no remote config, no A/B machinery (yet).
- Machine verification: every gameplay/rendering change re-runs a bot that
  beats all 40 levels at par through the real engine plus ~80 regression
  checks; an iOS UI-test bot does the same on-device.
- Analytics: an anonymous, batched beacon exists but ships disabled.


10. DESIGN CONSTRAINTS THE RESEARCHER SHOULD TREAT AS FIXED
-----------------------------------------------------------
1. 3-second sound-off legibility of any screen.
2. One verb (drag); no mid-play UI chrome.
3. Deterministic, solver-verified levels; no dynamic difficulty
   adjustment, no post-hoc outcome manipulation, no fake near-misses.
4. Shape cues wherever color carries meaning.
5. No paid randomness; no purchase celebration; ledger transparency.
6. The blueprint art direction is the identity — gamification proposals
   should be expressible in drafting-sheet vernacular (stamps, revisions,
   sheet numbers, approval seals, red-line markup...).

Everything else — including whether lives ever come back, the certification
economy, the survey's shape, session pacing — is open to challenge.


11. LINEAGE & SELF-ASSESSED POSITIONING (FOR THE RESEARCHER TO CORRECT)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Nearest neighbors as we understand them:
- Color Block Jam (Rollic) — closest mechanical relative: colored blocks
  dragged out through colored gates. Key differences on our side: strict
  one-drag-=-one-move economy with par/stars (CBJ is untimed-move-free with
  a countdown timer), polyomino variety incl. L-shapes, no timer anywhere,
  deterministic levels, and the blueprint identity vs. toy-plastic look.
- Block Blast / Unpuzzle / "unpack the grid" family — shares the
  clear-the-board satisfaction, differs in the gate/color routing logic.
- Parking Jam / Screw Jam / unscrew-and-sort wave — shares the
  "declutter a jammed space" fantasy and the rescue-at-fail monetization
  surface.
- Rush Hour (classic) — the sliding-blocks-in-a-crowded-grid ancestor,
  but Rush Hour slides on rails; Gate Escape routes freely around corners,
  which changes the entire solution texture (route-finding, not shunting).
The first "ownable twist" has SHIPPED: blocks that must exit in a stamped
sequence (the approval chain, Sheet 4). Still unbuilt: gates that change
color after each use; gates that close after N exits.


12. WHAT WE KNOW WE DON'T KNOW
------------------------------
- No live retention or funnel data exists (analytics disabled, no store
  release). All difficulty/retention tuning is from design convention and
  bot-verified par, not player telemetry.
- The lives system's net effect is still unmeasured; rather than ship an
  unmeasured friction we defaulted it OFF (section 7). If it ever returns it
  should return as a measured experiment, not as a default.
- The silent streak lapse is untested on real players: with the repair card
  gone, nobody has watched a player notice their streak is missing. This is
  the single riskiest copy decision of the simplify pass.
- The recent menu simplification moved the meta one tap deeper, and the
  2026-09-02 merge collapsed quests + streak + ladder into a single weekly
  Field Survey sheet; we accepted a possible engagement cost for a calmer
  first impression and a legible surface, and want a second opinion (Q7).
- The contract lock ("choose 2 of 4, set for the week once one earns
  progress") is untested. It could read as ownership of a real weekly plan,
  or as a trap sprung by a tap the player made before understanding it. It is
  the riskiest new rule of the merge and the next critic session covers it.


13. QUESTIONS FOR THE RESEARCHER
--------------------------------
Q1. Positioning: against the current top-grossing/top-download block and
    sorting puzzlers (Color Block Jam, Block Blast, Screw Jam wave,
    Hexa Sort, etc.), which of our differences are commercially meaningful
    and which are noise? Is "no timer + strict move-par economy" a
    differentiator or a retention liability in this genre?
Q2. Which trending mechanics from adjacent genres would hybridize cleanly
    with the one-drag/gate-alignment core under our constraints (sec. 10)?
    We are especially interested in mechanics that deepen routing (the
    around-corners drag) rather than add layers of board furniture.
Q3. Of our three ownable-twist candidates (color-cycling gates, closing
    gates, sequenced exits), which best matches proven engagement patterns,
    and what does the literature/market say about "rule that changes as
    you use it" mechanics in casual play?
Q4. Gamification audit: given sections 7-8, what proven systems are we
    missing that do NOT violate section 10 (e.g., collections/albums,
    seasonal sheets, mastery badges, spectate/share moments, daily puzzle
    with a global identical board, "perfect sheet" meta-goals)? Rank by
    expected retention impact for a solo-dev product.
Q5. Which of our shipped systems is most likely over-engineered or
    redundant for our stage (quests vs streak vs ladder vs chests), and
    what would you cut or merge first?
Q6. Lives: for a premium-feeling, no-timer puzzle with rescue ads at fail,
    does a lives system add or destroy value? Evidence either way?
Q7. First-session design: given the calm cover-sheet open (sec. 5), what
    does current evidence say about meta-system discovery — should quests/
    streak surface passively on the cover sheet (a status line), or is
    tap-deep discovery fine at our stage?
Q8. Difficulty: our spike is placed at L20-25 by genre convention. Any
    evidence on optimal spike placement/steepness for D1/D7 in move-limited
    (not timed) puzzlers?
Q9. Any monetization surfaces we have not considered that respect the
    hard rules in section 8 (no paid randomness, no purchase celebration,
    full ledger transparency)?
