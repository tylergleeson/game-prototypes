0. WHAT IT IS, IN ONE BREATH
----------------------------
A single-finger block-escape puzzle drawn as an architect's blueprint. Coloured
polyomino blocks sit on a small grid; coloured gates sit on the walls. Drag a block —
any distance, around corners — and it escapes when it reaches a wall gate of its own
colour that spans every lane it occupies. One drag is one move, no matter how far.
Clear every block within the level's move limit; hit the solver-proven minimum ("par")
for three stars. There is no clock anywhere. 40 levels in four "sheets" of ten, a new
identical-for-everyone Daily Draft every day, one weekly meta sheet (the Field Survey),
cosmetic certification rewards, and a first mechanic twist on Sheet 4: blocks stamped
with approval numbers that must leave in order. Native iOS (installed via developer
signing today; not yet on the App Store), a shareable single-file web build, no
monetization, no accounts, no server.


1. WHO USES IT — USER TYPES AND USE CASES
-----------------------------------------
1.1 Players (the product)
- FIRST-TIME PLAYER: opens to a calm cover sheet with three taps (Play / Levels / How to
  play) and a vignette of a block gliding out of its gate. Level 1 has one block, L1–3
  auto-show a ghost route, and the first four levels allow four spare moves. Meta systems
  are hidden until earned (see 5.6). Use case: understand the verb in under a minute,
  clear a sheet in ~10 minutes.
- RETURNING PLAYER: Continue takes them straight to the next level; from the first
  return day the cover shows a passive status line (draft filed? survey days stamped?).
  Use case: a 3–10 minute session — today's Daily Draft, a couple of campaign levels,
  a survey stamp.
- MASTERY PLAYER ("par chaser"): 3★ requires exactly optimal play; 2★ is within one of
  par; replay-for-★★★ is offered on any non-perfect win; the live star meter shows what
  is still attainable mid-level; undo is free. Use case: certify sheets (24★ of 30) to
  unlock papers and the Approval stamp; beat L20 (the exam) at par.
- DAILY PLAYER: one board a day, same for everyone, own par, one recorded attempt then
  unlimited practice; a spoiler-free FIELD REPORT to share. Use case: appointment play
  and light social comparison without leaderboards.
- SHARER: copies/shares the FIELD REPORT text (system share sheet on iOS, clipboard on
  web). It never contains the solution.
- ACCESSIBILITY USERS: colour is never the only signal (every colour has a printed symbol
  on blocks and gates); reduced motion (system setting or in-game toggle) shortens or
  halves every animation; sound and haptics are independent toggles; every screen is
  designed to be read in 3 seconds with sound off.
1.2 Internal users (the way the product is made)
- THE FOUNDER/LEAD (one person): makes taste and scope decisions, verifies every change
  independently, commits, installs to the phone, owns accounts and money.
- DEVELOPER AGENTS: AI subagents that change source under a written spec, must leave the
  bot green, and write a report; they never commit.
- CRITIC PERSONA ("Juno Adler"): an AI first-play reviewer that plays the real build in a
  visible iPhone-sized window, narrates, files notes and a scored review.
- BREAKER PERSONA ("Mara Voss"): an adversarial-QA AI that only hunts bugs (raw gestures,
  races, persistence, exploits) and files REPRO/EXPECTED/ACTUAL reports.
- BOTS (not agents): a headless browser bot that beats all 40 levels at par through the
  real engine plus 109 regression checks; an in-app iOS bot verified by XCUITest.
- MARKETING WORKERS: scripts (and an AI persona for TikTok batches) that record real
  gameplay into tours, trailers and vertical ads; nothing is filmed by hand.
- LATER: App Store reviewer; external playtesters via TestFlight; a researcher (you).

3. CORE PLAY — RULES, CONTROLS, FEEL, HELP
------------------------------------------
3.1 Board and pieces
- Grids from 4×5 (L1) to 7×9; cells hold a block cell, a stone (immovable 1×1), or
  nothing.
- Shapes: 1×1, 1×2, 2×1, 1×3, 3×1, 2×2 square, and four L-triomino orientations
  (ten shapes total).
- Colours and symbols (never colour alone): red ● circle, cyan ▲ triangle, green ◆
  diamond, amber ★ star. Up to four colours per level.
- Gates: coloured tabs on the outer walls, 1–4 lanes wide, with the colour's symbol.
3.2 The exit rule (checked cell-exactly)
- A block exits only through a gate of its own colour, only when flush against that
  wall, and only when the gate covers EVERY lane the block occupies. No partial or
  misaligned exits exist in the rules.
- Approval chain (Sheet 4): a block with a stamp number may exit only while its number
  is the lowest still on the board. Movement is never gated — only exits. Undo restores
  the chain automatically (the rule is derived from which blocks remain, not stored).
3.3 Movement and the move economy
- One finger, one verb: drag. The block walks cell-by-cell under the finger through free
  space, turning corners; it never passes through blocks, stones or walls. Reaching a
  valid gate mid-drag exits it. Releasing ends the move. ONE DRAG = ONE MOVE regardless
  of distance or corners. A drag that ends where it started costs nothing.
- Each level has a move LIMIT and a PAR (solver-proven minimum). Stars: 3 at ≤ par
  (i.e. exactly optimal — par cannot be beaten), 2 at par+1, 1 otherwise. The HUD star
  meter shows the stars still attainable given moves spent and blocks left.
- Limits: par+4 on L1–4, par+3 on L5–10, par+2 from L11 onward — with one exception:
  L31–32 par+4 and L33–34 par+3 while the approval chain is being taught; par+2 again
  from L35. Daily drafts are always par+3.
3.4 Help systems
- UNDO: one step, refunds the move, restores the whole board (and the chain), free and
  unlimited. Using it ends the exit pitch-chain.
- HINT ("?"): the in-engine solver computes the next optimal move and ghosts its exact
  drag route (or parking spot) on the blueprint until the board changes. It respects the
  approval chain. It is behind the ad slot. If no solution exists from here, the toast
  says so.
- RESCUE: on failure, +3 moves once per attempt via the ad slot; the board state is
  preserved (never re-dealt).
- RESTART: resets the level; on a recorded daily attempt it closes the record as NOT
  CLEARED (the fail sheet says so first).
3.5 Feel (all timings from the engine)
- Pickup: a 70 ms press dip; per-cell haptic ticks while walking (rate-limited); a damped
  snap on release.
- Exit choreography: the rendered block walks the exact cell path the logic took (34 ms
  per cell, catch-up bounded at 260 ms so a long flick never lags the finger); at the
  aligned cell the gate's lanes and the block's footprint flash (0.34 s, ink-coloured per
  paper), a 90 ms held beat, then the flight with a particle burst, a screen shake and a
  rising pitch — consecutive exits within ~4 s climb in pitch (a combo feel with no score).
  Reduced motion: 13 ms/cell, 100 ms lag, 40 ms hold, 0.2 s flash, half the particles,
  no shake.
- Sound kinds: tap, exit (three-variant climbing), hint, cert, gate (a gate "closes"
  with a flash when its last block leaves), star, undo, win, fail. Haptic beats on iOS:
  pick, step, settle, exit (a Core Haptics "whoosh"), win, low (moves), fail.

4.2 How the curve is shaped
- Difficulty is measured as par minus block count — the drags the best line spends
  repositioning rather than clearing. L16–30 runs 1 1 2 2 3 0 1 1 2 2 1 2 2 1 2: L20 is
  the strict local maximum (an insight level, not a bigger pile: the only 6×7 board, six
  of seven blocks corked at the opening, two colours queueing on one edge), L21–22 are
  relief, L23–25 a second rise cresting below the exam, L26–30 recombine taught ideas.
  The bot asserts this profile so a future retune cannot flatten it.
- Introduction schedule: one block (L1) → two colours (L2) → longer blocks (L3–4) →
  stones (L5) → a third colour (L8) → corking/"move twice" (Sheet 2) → L-shapes and
  squares mid-teens → a fourth colour and 7×9 boards (L17+) → approval chains (L31+),
  chain length 2 → 3 → 4, and the chain's cost in extra moves rising from 0 (teaching)
  to +3 by L40.
- Every level is generated by a spec (board size, colours, shapes allowed, block/stone
  counts, gate slack, required excess, structural flags such as "corked" or "shared
  side") and accepted only if the solver proves it solvable, its par lands in the spec's
  band, its hint cost stays under a latency ceiling (so the in-game hint answers in
  < ~150 ms on a phone), and — for chained levels — the chain genuinely costs moves
  versus the unchained board. Per-level seeds mean retuning one level leaves every other
  level byte-identical (the bot pins hashes of L1–15 and L16–30 separately).


5. META SYSTEMS
---------------
5.1 Stars and Sheet Certification
- Each level pays up to 3★; a sheet holds 30. At 24★ a sheet is CERTIFIED (the chapter
  chip flips from a dashed pending frame to a solid stamped frame; a "cert" sound; the
  win card shows "Sheet certified" + the reward).
- Rewards: Sheet 1 → Sepia draft paper; Sheet 2 → Night vellum; Sheet 3 → Whiteprint;
  Sheet 4 → the Approval stamp (a cosmetic seal inked on every win card thereafter).
  Deterministic and previewable; the word "chest" was retired because it implies
  randomness.
5.2 Papers (themes)
- Four papers re-ink the entire drawing (canvas and chrome) through tokens: Cyanotype
  (default), Sepia draft, Night vellum, Whiteprint. Every surface — scrims, shadows,
  legend rules, HUD chips, the alignment flash — is tokenised and machine-checked per
  paper for contrast floors (≥ 4.5:1 on key chrome). Selected from the sheet index or
  the pause sheet.
5.3 Daily Draft
- One board per calendar day, identical for every player, precomputed and
  solver-verified: 365 rows shipped starting 2026-09-01 (19.7 KB), seeded from the date
  string, with a weekday rhythm — Mon/Tue easy (6×8, 2 colours, 4 blocks), Wed/Sun mid
  (3 colours, 5 blocks, 1 stone), Thu/Fri hard (6 blocks, 2 stones), Sat peak (7×9, 4
  colours, 7 blocks). Limit is always par+3.
- Append-only guarantee: a lock file hashes every row up to today; regenerating the
  table aborts if any past row would change. Past a table's end, the runtime maps the
  date onto a verified row rather than generating anything.
- Play rules: the draft row appears after the third campaign clear. Tapping READY shows
  the pre-board card stating that the first attempt is the recorded one. The record
  CLOSES on the first resolution: a win, or a failure resolved by Retry/leaving the board
  (the rescue keeps the attempt open and is recorded as a fact). Afterwards the day is
  PRACTICE · NOT RECORDED forever (HUD, pause, win card all say so). Drafts never touch
  campaign progress, stars, the unlock pointer, certification or the Continue CTA (a
  regression check proves byte-identical campaign state before/during/after).
- FIELD REPORT (shareable, spoiler-free). Format:
    GATE ESCAPE · FIELD REPORT
    3 Sep 2026 · CLEARED
    ★★☆
    ■■■■■■■□
    8 moves / par 7 · 88% route
    undo 0 · hint 0
  The bar is par filled cells then (moves − par) open cells, capped at 20 with "+n";
  losses show "NOT CLEARED" with blocks out instead of the bar; "· rescued" is appended
  as a fact. Only the codepoints ★ ☆ ■ □ · plus ASCII are allowed (pinned by a check),
  and the text never contains coordinates or a move list — a rejected design was a
  per-move exit/park grid, which would leak the optimal line on a global board.
- Delivery: iOS share sheet → clipboard → a selectable text box, in that fallback order.
5.4 Field Survey (the one weekly meta sheet)
- Replaces the earlier daily quests + streak card + weekly ladder with one surface.
- The week (ISO week, Monday start) deterministically offers 4 of these 8 contracts:
  Clear 12 levels · Clear 20 levels · Earn 30 stars · Earn 45 stars · Clear 8 levels at
  par · Clear 5 levels without undo · Clear 8 levels without hints · Clear 60 blocks.
  The player takes 2; swapping is free until a taken contract earns progress, then the
  pair is set for the week.
- Day spine: any win (campaign or draft) stamps today.
- Points and marks: 1 point per clear, +1 at par; marks at 3, 7, 12, 20.
- Filing one contract banks a WEATHER DELAY (hold up to 2); filing both SEALS the week
  and yields a fragment (a collectible count).
- Missed day: if a delay is banked it is spent automatically and stamped "~" on the
  spine (a launch notice says so); with none banked the streak simply restarts — no
  modal, no ask, no repair ad.
- Week rollover keeps only the "last week" line. Existing saves migrated once from the
  old quest/ladder keys; the streak record itself was never altered.
5.5 Streak
- Consecutive days with at least one clear; shown on the cover stamp line and the survey
  header; best-ever kept; "New best streak" surfaces as a quiet win-card row. There is no
  streak repair purchase or ad (deleted on purpose).
5.6 Staged first-run (progressive disclosure)
- The sheet index opens bare. Reveals are derived from progress, not stored flags:
  Sheet Certification (+ paper picker) after 2 clears; Daily Draft after 3; Field Survey
  after 5 — with the EASIEST offered contract preselected as a worked example (ranked by
  expected clears; never a contract that can score zero on a clear). Each reveal
  announces itself exactly once, as a quiet NEW row painted with the win card:
  "Sheet certification · 24 ★ on a sheet earns its paper", "Daily draft · One board a
  day, the same for everyone", "Field survey · A week to fill in · one contract taken".
- From the first return day the cover carries the passive status line (≤ 2 facts:
  "Today's draft is filed" · "3 of 7 survey days").
- First failure teaches the rescue once. The approval-chain legend row appears once
  Sheet 4 is reachable.
5.7 Lives (dormant, flag-gated)
- Shipped OFF by default after the research round. When enabled (?lives=1 or a flag in
  local storage): 5 lives, L1–5 free, a failed retry from L6 costs one, the rescue
  preserves the attempt, one life refills per 25 minutes, full refill via the ad slot, a
  hearts row in the HUD and sheet index, an out-of-lives card. Fully regression-tested
  in both states.
5.8 Settings and toggles
- Sound, Haptics (native), Motion (reduce animations) — each persisted; the system
  reduce-motion preference is also honoured. Reset progress (two-tap). No account, no
  cloud save, no notifications.


6. DATA MODEL AND DETERMINISM
-----------------------------
- Everything lives in the device's local storage (WebView localStorage on iOS):
  ge_prog {u unlock pointer, s[] stars per level, skin, skins[], appr, seen[], rv[]
  reveals played, d0 first-clear date} · ge_level (resume) · ge_best (best per level) ·
  ge_streak {len, best, lastDate, freezes, marks[]} · ge_survey {week, offered[],
  chosen[], prog{}, filed[], days{}, delays[], pts, ms[], seal, frags, last} ·
  ge_daily {cur{date,state,moves,par,stars,undos,hints,rescued,cleared}, practice,
  hist[≤30]} · ge_sound · ge_haptics · ge_motion · ge_tips (one-shot tips shown) ·
  ge_stats (event counters) · ge_flags (dormant systems) · ge_lives · ge_iid (anonymous
  install id for the disabled beacon).
- Nothing is random at runtime: levels and dailies are static verified data; contract
  offers are seeded from the week; the only randomness is particle decoration.
- The engine exposes a test surface (window.GE / GE_MENU: read state, act through the
  finger's code path, override the clock, load any level/daily/test board, ask the
  solver) that bots and reviewer personas drive — the same hooks the player's finger
  ultimately calls.


7. ACCESSIBILITY AND LEGIBILITY GUARANTEES (hard rules)
-------------------------------------------------------
- 3-second sound-off legibility of every screen.
- Colour never carries meaning alone: symbols on blocks and gates; certification states,
  next-up stamps and paper swatches all differ by shape or fill, not hue.
- Everything the player can act on has solid fill plus outline.
- Reduced motion (system or toggle) shortens or halves every animation; nothing is
  skipped that carries information (the alignment flash still shows, briefly).
- Contrast floors per paper are machine-checked; text minimum sizes are enforced.
- One verb, no mid-play chrome, no timers anywhere.


8. MONETIZATION POSTURE
-----------------------
Nothing is for sale and no real ads run. Reserved surfaces already built as free
simulated ad slots: rescue (+3 moves at failure), hint (the next move), life refill
(dormant). Hard rules for whenever money ships: no celebratory feedback on a purchase
event; every currency grant/spend gets a ledger entry and any transaction UI shows
opening balance, cost, reward, closing balance; no win language on a net-negative
outcome; paid random rewards are permanently off the roadmap. The research round's
recommended launch model (not built): rewarded rescue + rewarded hint + a one-time
"Supporter Edition" (remove forced ads) + fixed cosmetic paper folios; later paid
expansion sheets. Deferred: fail packs, ad-skip tickets, subscriptions, passes.


9. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE
-------------------------
- Engine: zero-dependency HTML + canvas JavaScript (index.html, game.js ≈ engine,
  menu.js ≈ every screen outside the board, levels.js, dailies.js, beacon.js). No build
  step for development; three build targets: a single self-contained HTML file (used for
  the private shareable artifact), an itch.io zip, and app/www (installable PWA with a
  service worker + the Capacitor web directory).
- iOS: Capacitor shell (SwiftPM, no CocoaPods) around a WKWebView, with a native
  HapticsDriver (UIKit generators + one Core Haptics pattern), a launch-argument bot
  bridge (-autoplay runs the in-app bot and mirrors its status into an accessibility
  label), and a studio bridge (-studio <url>: the app polls a local console for
  JavaScript to run so reviewer personas can drive the real app in the Simulator).
  Privacy manifest and usage strings included. Installed to the founder's iPhone by
  developer signing (wireless once paired); TestFlight/App Store pending a unique store
  name ("Gate Escape" is taken; candidate "Gate Escape: Blueprint Puzzle").
- Build stamp: every build writes build-info.js with the local date-time; the cover
  sheet shows it as "REV YYYY-MM-DD · HH:MM" so any install or artifact identifies its
  build at a glance.
- Analytics: an anonymous batched beacon exists but ships disabled (BEACON_URL empty →
  the bot asserts zero network requests across a full run). Event catalogue when
  enabled: session_start, heartbeat (60 s while visible), level_start, win, fail,
  restart, retry, undo, hint, hint_none, hint_nudge, block_exit, seq_refused,
  rescue_offer_tap, rescue_used, ad_start, ad_done, replay, skin_select, cert_earned,
  streak_day, survey_day, survey_point, survey_mark, survey_seal, contract_select,
  contract_preselect, contract_filed, weather_delay_used, survey_migrated, daily_enter,
  daily_confirm, daily_confirm_back, daily_started/won/lost/practice, daily_report,
  daily_share, daily_shared, ftue_reveal, lives_* (dormant). No device identifiers, no
  third-party SDKs. Planned endpoint: a tiny collector on the founder's other product's
  API; missing today: a streak-lapse event.


10. HOW IT IS BUILT, TESTED AND REVIEWED (the "factory")
-------------------------------------------------------
This is the part we most want benchmarked. One founder plus AI agents in fixed roles,
with machine verification as the gate for every change.
10.1 Content pipeline
- tools/gen-core.mjs holds the ONE tool-side copy of the rules (shapes, movement, exit,
  chain), the generator and the breadth-first solver; generate.mjs holds the 40-level
  curve spec + per-level seeds; solve-paths.mjs records optimal action replays
  (solutions.json). generate-dailies.mjs produces the year of drafts with the lock.
  Regenerating with unchanged specs is byte-identical (asserted).
10.2 The commit gate
- node tools/playtest.mjs: a headless Chromium bot replays every level's optimal solution
  through the real engine and asserts moves/limit/par per level, then runs 109 named
  regression checks — one per shipped feature or fixed bug — covering star bands, the
  limits schedule, the sawtooth profile, certification (4 sheets), papers and contrast,
  undo/rescue interplay, hint solver + latency, exit-glide legality (0 illegal frames),
  reduced motion, all tips, the ad slot's grant-only-on-completion rule, scrim/Escape
  dismissal rules, the landing's exactly-3-interactive-elements contract, staged
  disclosure walk with a real day+1 reload, survey roll/select/lock/migration/spine/
  delays/seal/rollover, daily determinism across two browser contexts, record-once,
  loss/rescue semantics, share-text regex + codepoint allowlist + spoiler assertion,
  par replay of sampled drafts, table fallback, lock hash, bundle size, approval-chain
  rule checks (illegal exit refused, chain advance, undo restores order, hint legality)
  plus a rule-parity oracle (200 random reachable positions compared engine-vs-tools:
  zero mismatches), the fail-headline bands on purpose-built boards, "bundles fresh"
  (byte-exact staleness check of all three build outputs), the beacon-off no-network
  assertion, and store-screenshot capture. Runtime ≈ 2 minutes.
- tools/playtest-ios.sh: rebuilds app/www, syncs the shell, runs an XCUITest that launches
  the app with -autoplay; the in-app bot dismisses any launch notice, plays all 40
  levels, exercises the rescue, and reports BOT PASS 40/40; screenshots are exported;
  simulators are shut down.
- A change ships only when both are green; the lead re-runs the web bot independently
  before every commit and reads the new screenshots.
10.3 Personas and the review loop
- A local studio console drives the real build (Chromium frame or the iOS Simulator) and
  exposes a tiny API: look (state + screenshot), say (narration + a note in a fixed
  schema: area, severity, text), act (drag/tap/hint/wait; for the breaker also raw
  gestures, tap bursts, sequences, key events, reload, inspect), end (files review.md,
  notes.json, log.md).
- CRITIC sessions: first-play reviews against the hybrid-casual bar, budgeted by level
  range or minutes, run singly or three in parallel on identical Simulator copies laid
  out side by side, each with its own commentary panel.
- BREAKER sessions: adversarial QA with a written attack plan (input abuse, timing races,
  state consistency, economy exploits, persistence, every screen's every button).
- DEVELOPER PASS: one pass consumes every report: triage each note DO NOW / DESIGN
  CHANGE / SKIP (with reasons), reproduce through the hooks before fixing, fix root
  causes, add a regression check per fix, verify with both bots, write dev-report.md;
  the lead verifies, commits, republishes.
- Research → rules: external research is turned into an audit table, then into
  bright-line rules in the repo's instructions file so later passes cannot regress them.
10.4 A worked example: the research round (2026-09-02)
- Input: your first report. Output, in one evening: 9 passes (tooling refactor with a
  byte-identical proof; lives off + certification rename; Field Survey merge with
  migration; Daily Draft engine + data; staged first-run + draft UI; approval-chain
  engine + rendering; Sheet 4 generation with hint-latency gating; the sawtooth reshape
  with tighter limits; collateral + iOS bot) → a critic session (7/10; 20 notes) → a
  developer pass (17 fixed, 1 design change, 1 skipped with measured evidence, 1
  deferred pending data) → fresh install to the founder's phone. Cross-checks between
  agents caught five integration bugs before commit; the personas' "not reproducible"
  verdicts were backed by measurements, not opinion. Regression checks grew from 68 to
  109.
10.5 Model economics
- Lead on the top model; developers/personas on the mid tier; exploration and documents
  on the light tier; escalate a failed pass one tier up rather than loop.

13. QUESTIONS FOR THE RESEARCHER
--------------------------------
MARKET
Q1. Given the full feature map above, which current apps are the closest analogues on
    each axis — core verb (free-routing block escape), meta (daily identical puzzle +
    weekly contracts + certification cosmetics), and posture (no timer, no lives, no
    randomness) — and what do their public metrics say about each axis?
Q2. Which features in state-of-the-art casual puzzle apps (2025–2026) would slot into
    this design without breaking section 7's guarantees or the no-randomness contract —
    ranked by expected retention value for a solo developer?
Q3. Daily-puzzle design: what do the best implementations (Wordle-class dailies, NYT
    Games, Puzzmo, Linkedin games, etc.) do about first-attempt recording, practice
    modes, spoiler-safe sharing, and archive access that we should adopt or avoid?
Q4. Weekly contract/"choose 2 of 4" systems: evidence on choice-driven weekly goals vs
    fixed daily quests for retention and perceived autonomy.
Q5. The approval-chain mechanic: precedents for order-constrained clearing in casual
    puzzles, and evidence on how far a rule-changing sheet can go before comprehension
    drops.
REVIEW PROCESS (section 10)
Q6. How does our verification stack (solver-proven levels, 109 regression checks, an
    in-app iOS bot, AI critic/breaker personas, one developer pass per round) compare
    with how best-in-class studios test and tune? What do they do that we don't —
    e.g. telemetry-driven difficulty tuning, funnel instrumentation standards, cohort
    A/B on level order, human playtest panels, accessibility audits, crash/ANR gates,
    LiveOps cadence, release trains?
Q7. What is state of the art for AI-assisted QA and playtesting in games today
    (automated agents, synthetic players, difficulty predictors, UX heuristics
    checkers)? Which tools or papers should we look at, and which of our checks are
    naive by comparison?
Q8. What telemetry event model and KPIs would a top studio consider the minimum for a
    soft launch of a game like this (beyond our section 9 catalogue), and what
    thresholds mark "kill", "iterate", "scale"?
Q9. Persona reviews: how do experienced studios structure heuristic/expert review so
    findings are reproducible and prioritised? Is our note schema (area, severity,
    text; REPRO/EXPECTED/ACTUAL for bugs) missing anything that makes triage better?
Q10. What would you change about the ORDER we iterate — research → rules → passes →
    critic → developer pass — for the next cycle, and what should the next cycle's
    single most important measurement be?
